0. DATA, EQUATION, NORMS, AND ANALYTIC INPUTS

Let
  D_A = {a in S(R^3;R^3) : div a = 0},
and fix viscosity nu > 0. Consider the unforced equations
  partial_t u + (u . grad)u + grad p = nu Delta u,
  div u = 0,
  u(0) = a.

All spatial norms are over R^3 with ordinary Lebesgue measure. Pointwise
vector norms are Euclidean and pointwise matrix/tensor norms are Frobenius.
There is no mean-zero condition and no periodic spectral-gap constant.

Write
  E(t) = ||u(t)||_2^2,
  Y(t) = ||grad u(t)||_2^2,
  Z(t) = ||Delta u(t)||_2^2,
  E_0  = ||a||_2^2,
  Y_0  = ||grad a||_2^2.

Let U be the unique maximal H^1 strong/mild solution from a, with
maximal lifetime T_max in (0,+infinity]. On each compact subinterval of
[0,T_max), it is continuous into H^1 and belongs to L^2_t H^2_x.
For Schwartz initial data, U and its normalized pressure are smooth,
including the initial time. The pressure can be taken as
  p = sum_{i,j} R_i R_j(U_i U_j),
up to an arbitrary smooth function of time.

The established analytic inputs used are:
(A0) Local H^1 existence, uniqueness and positive-time smoothing on R^3.
(A1) H^1 continuation: bounded H^1 norm up to a finite maximal time
     excludes that time as a terminal lifespan.
(A2) Global Leray–Hopf existence for L^2 solenoidal data, with the
     strong energy inequality at almost every starting time, including 0.
(A3) Weak–strong uniqueness from admissible restart times.
(A4) For the literal smooth finite-energy Fefferman solution category,
     bounded-enstrophy and uniqueness results identify such solutions
     with the canonical H^1 solution on closed finite slabs.

These are analytic theorems, not hypothetical universal regularity inputs.
Useful sources and exact theorem locations are collected in Section 12.

Precise LH convention used throughout:
LH_nu(a) consists of global distributional solenoidal solutions with
  u in C_w([0,infinity);L^2_sigma)
       intersect L^infinity(0,infinity;L^2)
       intersect L^2(0,infinity;dot H^1),
strong L^2 attainment of the initial datum, and
  ||u(t)||_2^2 + 2nu integral_s^t ||grad u(r)||_2^2 dr
    <= ||u(s)||_2^2
for all t>=s, for s=0 and a full-measure set of positive starting times.
Use the weakly continuous representative. At such a starting time,
weak continuity and the energy inequality give right strong L^2 continuity.
An energy inequality only from 0 would not suffice for the restart argument.


1. E01 — FIX THE WHOLE-SPACE SOBOLEV CONSTANT

Let S_6 be any constant for which
  ||f||_6 <= S_6 ||grad f||_2
holds on R^3.

The same S_6 applies to vector/tensor fields: apply the scalar inequality
to their Euclidean norm, use |grad |f|| <= |grad f| almost everywhere,
and pass by density where needed.

For the numerical versions in this record, choose Talenti's constant
  S_6 = 2^(2/3) / (sqrt(3) pi^(2/3)).
Then
  S_6^6 = 16 / (27 pi^4).

The scalar extremal f(x)=(1+|x|^2)^(-1/2) checks this normalization:
  ||f||_6^6 = pi^2/4,
  ||grad f||_2^2 = 3 pi^2/4.
Talenti's theorem establishes that the resulting ratio is an admissible
sharp scalar Sobolev constant. No sharpness is asserted for the later
Navier–Stokes estimates.

We will also use the whole-space identity
  ||D^2 u||_2 = ||Delta u||_2,
which follows componentwise from Plancherel.

STATUS E01: established analytic input with explicit normalization.


2. E02 — ENSTROPHY DIFFERENTIAL INEQUALITY

For a sufficiently regular incompressible solution, testing with -Delta u
gives the enstrophy identity and hence
  (1/2) Y' + nu Z
    <= |integral_R3 (u . grad)u . Delta u dx|.

Holder, interpolation, Sobolev and the Hessian identity give
  |integral (u . grad)u . Delta u|
    <= ||u||_6 ||grad u||_3 ||Delta u||_2
    <= S_6 ||grad u||_2
         ||grad u||_2^(1/2) ||grad u||_6^(1/2)
         ||Delta u||_2
    <= S_6^(3/2) Y^(3/4) Z^(3/4).

Young's inequality with the indicated dissipation split gives
  S_6^(3/2) Y^(3/4) Z^(3/4)
    <= (nu/2) Z + (27/32) S_6^6 nu^(-3) Y^3.

Therefore
  Y' + nu Z <= C_E nu^(-3) Y^3,
where
  C_E = (27/16) S_6^6 = pi^(-4).

For H^1 strong solutions the integrated form follows by approximation
and positive-time regularity. The energy estimate controls the L^2 part
of the H^1 norm throughout.

STATUS E02: derived.


3. E03 — CONSTRUCT THE INITIAL WINDOW FROM EACH DATUM

For a nonzero a in D_A, one has Y_0 > 0: an L^2 field with zero gradient
would be a spatial constant and hence would vanish.

Put
  c = C_E nu^(-3).
The inequality Y' <= c Y^3 yields the comparison
  Y(t) <= Y_0 / sqrt(1 - 2 c Y_0^2 t)
as long as the solution exists and the denominator is positive.
The comparison is valid through a zero value of Y by the zero solution
and uniqueness, or by an elementary positive regularization.

Define
  T_loc(a,nu) = nu^3 / (4 C_E ||grad a||_2^4)
              = pi^4 nu^3 / (4 ||grad a||_2^4).

On the interval in question the denominator is at least 1/sqrt(2), so
  Y(t) <= sqrt(2) Y_0                    for 0 <= t <= T_loc,
or equivalently
  ||grad U(t)||_2 <= 2^(1/4)||grad a||_2.

To justify existence on the CLOSED interval, suppose T_max <= T_loc.
The comparison and the energy estimate bound the H^1 norm uniformly
on [0,T_max). Input (A1) extends the solution, contradicting maximality.
Thus
  T_max > T_loc.

For a=0, use U=0 and p=0, and assign any chosen finite positive window
(for example T_loc=1 in the chosen time units). The zero case does not
require a reciprocal formula.

If a finite cap H>0 is desired uniformly in the definition, use
  T_loc,H(a,nu) = min{H,T_loc(a,nu)}
for nonzero a, and T_loc,H(0,nu)=H. All conclusions remain true.

STATUS E03: derived; unconditional for every admissible datum.


4. E04 — THE ACTUAL FINITE-TIME A STATEMENT

For every nu>0 and every a in D_A, the elementwise construction above gives
a finite T_loc(a,nu)>0 and velocity/pressure fields on
  R^3 x [0,T_loc(a,nu)]
with all of the following properties:

  (i)   u and p are smooth, including the initial time;
  (ii)  the unforced Navier–Stokes equations hold;
  (iii) div u = 0 and u(0)=a;
  (iv)  ||u(t)||_2^2 + 2nu integral_0^t ||grad u(s)||_2^2 ds = E_0;
  (v)   ||grad u(t)||_2^2 <= sqrt(2)||grad a||_2^2.

The solution is unique in the H^1 strong class; pressure is fixed modulo
an additive smooth function of time. Its existence follows from local theory,
and E02–E03 give the explicit guaranteed lifespan.

There is also an explicit finite-window charge bound:
  integral_0^T_loc ||grad u(t)||_2^4 dt
    <= integral_0^T_loc Y_0^2/(1-2cY_0^2t) dt
    = (nu^3/(2C_E)) log 2
    = (pi^4 nu^3/2) log 2.

The zero solution satisfies this bound as well.

This is a genuine weakening of the time requirement in Fefferman A.
The solution is classical on its entire constructed window.
"Elementwise" means that each datum a is assigned its own time by a
displayed norm formula. No global regularity assumption is used to
define or justify that initial window. Evaluation of an arbitrary
abstract datum's norms is not asserted to be an effective algorithm.

STATUS E04: finite-time A endpoint established at B-tier.


5. E05 — A SMALL ENERGY-ENSTROPHY PRODUCT GIVES GLOBAL CONTINUATION

A second estimate of the same nonlinear term is useful. By interpolation,
  ||u||_3 <= ||u||_2^(1/2)||u||_6^(1/2)
           <= S_6^(1/2) E^(1/4)Y^(1/4),
and
  ||grad u||_6 <= S_6 Z^(1/2).
Consequently
  |integral (u . grad)u . Delta u|
    <= ||u||_3 ||grad u||_6 ||Delta u||_2
    <= S_6^(3/2)(E Y)^(1/4) Z.

It follows that
  (1/2)Y' + [nu-S_6^(3/2)(E Y)^(1/4)]Z <= 0.

Define the strict threshold
  theta(nu) = nu^4/S_6^6
            = (27 pi^4/16)nu^4.

Suppose an H^1 strong solution is started at time s from a datum b with
  P_s = ||b||_2^2 ||grad b||_2^2 < theta(nu).
Set
  delta_s = nu - S_6^(3/2)P_s^(1/4) > 0.

On the bootstrap interval where E Y < theta, both E and Y are
nonincreasing. Hence E(t)Y(t) <= P_s and
  Y(t) + 2delta_s integral_s^t Z(r)dr <= Y(s).
Continuity prevents exit from that region. The H^1 norm stays bounded,
so (A1) rules out a finite maximal time.

Thus every divergence-free H^1 datum satisfying
  ||b||_2^2 ||grad b||_2^2 < nu^4/S_6^6
has a global strong continuation, smooth at every positive time after
the restart. The strict inequality supplies a fixed positive margin;
no equality-threshold conclusion is needed.

STATUS E05: derived global continuation for this explicit subclass.


6. E06 — AN INTEGRATED PRODUCT BOUND FROM ENERGY ALONE

For a global Leray–Hopf solution v, write E,Y for its energy and
enstrophy at the almost-everywhere times where these are defined.
Its energy inequality gives
  E(t) + 2nu integral_0^t Y(r)dr <= E_0.

Define the absolutely continuous function
  A(t) = E_0 - 2nu integral_0^t Y(r)dr.
Then
  0 <= E(t) <= A(t),      A' = -2nu Y
almost everywhere. Therefore, for every finite T,
  integral_0^T E(t)Y(t)dt
    <= integral_0^T A(t)Y(t)dt
    = [E_0^2-A(T)^2]/(4nu)
    <= E_0^2/(4nu).

The same argument applies to a strong solution on every interval on
which it exists. It does not require fractional Sobolev estimates.

STATUS E06: derived.


7. E07 — CONSTRUCT AN EXPLICIT EVENTUAL-REGULARITY CUTOFF

For a nonzero a, define
  tau_A(a,nu) = E_0^2/(2nu theta(nu))
              = S_6^6 ||a||_2^4/(2nu^5)
              = [8/(27pi^4)] ||a||_2^4/nu^5.

This depends only on the datum and viscosity.
The value is a safe explicit constant for the argument; it is not
claimed optimal.

E06 implies
  integral_0^tau_A E(t)Y(t)dt <= (theta tau_A)/2.
By the elementary measure estimate for a nonnegative function,
  measure{t in (0,tau_A): E(t)Y(t) >= theta} <= tau_A/2.
Hence
  measure{t in (0,tau_A): E(t)Y(t) < theta} >= tau_A/2 > 0.

For a Leray–Hopf solution, intersect this positive-measure set with
the full-measure set of admissible restart times. At such a time s:
  - v(s) is in H^1 and is solenoidal;
  - the energy inequality can be restarted at s;
  - the representative has the right L^2 trace required for (A3).

Choose such an s. Then 0<s<tau_A and E(s)Y(s)<theta.
By E05, the trace v(s) generates a global strong solution.
By weak–strong uniqueness, that continuation agrees with v after s.
It is smooth for every time strictly greater than s.

Consequently every Leray–Hopf solution from a has a smooth
representative on
  R^3 x [tau_A(a,nu),infinity).
The endpoint tau_A itself is included because s<tau_A.

For a=0, set tau_A=0. The energy inequality forces v=0, so the
conclusion is immediate.

The quantifier is "for every specified Leray–Hopf solution."
The selected restart time may depend on that solution. This does
not assert uniqueness across an earlier potentially singular interval.

STATUS E07: unconditional eventual regularity, with an explicit cutoff.
This is consistent with the classical whole-space regularity-time
scaling C nu^(-5)||a||_2^4 in source [3].


8. E08 — DEFINE THE PRECISE FINITE-WINDOW BUDGET

Return to the canonical maximal strong solution U from a. Let
  b = min{T_max(a,nu),tau_A(a,nu)}.

For a nonzero a define the nonnegative improper integral
  J_A(a,nu)
    = lim_{r up to b} integral_0^r ||grad U(t)||_2^4 dt
    in [0,infinity].
For a=0 set J_A=0.

If tau_A<T_max this is just the ordinary integral on [0,tau_A].
If T_max<=tau_A, the limit includes possible divergence at T_max.

It is essential to require finiteness of the full improper integral.
Finiteness on every compact subinterval of [0,T_max) would be automatic
and would not be a continuation criterion.

The cutoff is assigned from a alone. The value of J_A depends on the
evolution. A formula for tau_A is not a proof that J_A is finite.

STATUS E08: precise definition, including terminal endpoint conventions.


9. E09 — FINITE BUDGET FORCES CROSSING OF THE CUTOFF

E02 implies
  Y' <= C_E nu^(-3) Y^2 Y.
Gronwall therefore gives, for t<T_max,
  Y(t) <= Y_0 exp[C_E nu^(-3) integral_0^t Y(r)^2dr].

Suppose J_A<infinity but T_max<=tau_A.
The preceding bound controls Y uniformly up to T_max, while energy
controls E. Input (A1) then extends the solution past T_max.
This contradicts maximality.

Therefore
  J_A(a,nu)<infinity  ==>  T_max(a,nu)>tau_A(a,nu).

The case T_max=tau_A is covered by the same argument. The conclusion
is strict, so no equality endpoint is lost.

STATUS E09: derived conditional continuation.


10. E10 — THE FINITE-WINDOW IMPLICATION TO GLOBAL A

For a=0 the global conclusion is immediate from the zero solution.
Assume a is nonzero and J_A<infinity for the following restart argument.
By E09, U exists beyond tau_A. Apply E06–E07
to this strong solution on [0,tau_A]. They supply a time s<tau_A
with
  ||U(s)||_2^2 ||grad U(s)||_2^2 < theta.

E05 supplies a global strong continuation from U(s).
Strong uniqueness identifies that continuation with U on their common
interval. Maximality now yields
  T_max(a,nu)=infinity.

For Schwartz datum, the resulting velocity and normalized pressure are
smooth on every finite closed time slab; the global energy bound is
  sup_{t>=0}||U(t)||_2^2 <= ||a||_2^2.
Thus the datumwise Fefferman-A conclusion follows.

Conversely, if T_max=infinity, continuity into H^1 on the compact
interval [0,tau_A] gives J_A<infinity. Therefore, in the canonical
strong formulation,
  J_A(a,nu)<infinity  <==>  T_max(a,nu)=infinity.

For the literal smooth finite-energy Fefferman solution class, the
standard identification on finite closed slabs also gives the converse:
source [2], Corollary 11.1 upgrades such a solution with H^1 datum
to an H^1 solution; Corollary 4.3 gives a normalized H^1 mild solution;
Theorem 5.4(iii) identifies its velocity with U. For Schwartz data,
Theorem 5.4(iv) supplies smoothness of the normalized pressure as well.
This uses equality of pressure gradients and does not presume smoothness
of a pressure-gauge function before the mild-solution identification.
The closed-slab premise is used only when an actual global smooth
solution is given for the converse, never at an unknown singular endpoint.
This is an analytic solution-class argument, not an assertion that an
existing Lean endpoint has been connected to that theorem.

Consequently, universal finiteness of J_A is a finite-window reformulation
of full Fefferman A. It is not an unconditionally established weakened
global regularity theorem.

The proof of the conditional implication itself uses strong restarts.
The separate Leray–Hopf argument in E07 explains the eventual tail and
does not have to be used as a gluing shortcut in E10.

STATUS E10: conditional root implication and datumwise equivalence derived.


11. E11 — WHAT IS PROVED, AND THE FIRST UNRESOLVED EDGE

The following finite-time statement is already established for every
admissible datum:
  a -> T_loc(a,nu)>0 -> smooth finite-energy solution on [0,T_loc].

The following eventual-time statement is also established:
  a -> tau_A(a,nu)<infinity -> every Leray–Hopf solution is smooth
  on [tau_A,infinity).

For smooth initial data, weak–strong uniqueness also identifies every
Leray–Hopf solution with the initial strong solution on [0,T_loc].
If T_loc>=tau_A, these controlled time intervals overlap and the
solution is globally regular.

For arbitrary data they need not overlap. When T_loc<tau_A, the closed
finite interval between these two explicitly constructed times contains
all time locations not covered by these two unconditional statements.
Its possible presence is not removed by the existence of the tail.

The remaining universal obligation is exactly
  for all nu>0 and all a in D_A, J_A(a,nu)<infinity.

FIRST UNRESOLVED EDGE
  known energy/product estimates
       -- unresolved -->
  finite integral_0^min(T_max,tau_A) ||grad U(t)||_2^4 dt
  for arbitrary admissible a.

Cause:
  energy controls integral Y;
  E06 additionally controls integral E Y;
  the continuation argument needs integral Y^2.
No general implication closing that integrability gap has been derived.

For a simple comparison of integrability powers, the scalar function
Y(t)=(1-t)^(-1/2) on [0,1) is integrable but its square is not.
This is only an integrability example, not a Navier–Stokes solution or
a proposed counterexample to regularity.

No unconditional global root conclusion is taken beyond this edge.
A next scoped endpoint could be to prove a finite J_A bound for a
specified admissible datum or a precisely defined additional class.
That suggestion is not adoption of a new project goal.

Relation to the recovered whole-space program:
the finite-window budget is an analytic sufficient input for its desired
global regularity exit. This record does not claim that a recovered
provider, port, payment estimate or covariance bound already supplies it.
A source-level/formal insertion would require matching the actual
solution class, norms, time domain and theorem interfaces.

Relation to the earlier periodic finite-window work:
the logical pattern is analogous, but every estimate here is on R^3.
No torus Poincare constant, discrete Fourier normalization, or mean-zero
condition was transported into these formulas.

Scaling check:
for a_lambda(x)=lambda a(lambda x),
  E_0(a_lambda)=lambda^(-1)E_0(a),
  Y_0(a_lambda)=lambda Y_0(a).
For nonzero data, both T_loc and tau_A scale as lambda^(-2), as required
by Navier–Stokes time scaling. The chosen finite local-window convention
for the zero solution is a separate harmless convention.

13. QFA / WFA — STRUCTURAL MATCH WITH QFB / WFB
Added: 2026-11-20

This addendum gives the whole-space statements the requested QFA/WFA
organization. It preserves the earlier periodic WFB naming convention:
  QFB = Quasi–Fefferman B,
  WFB = Weak Fourierman B (Weak_Fourierman_B).

The matching whole-space labels used here are:
  QFA = Quasi–Fefferman A,
  WFA = Weak Fourierman A (Weak_Fourierman_A).

WFA denotes the conditional finite-budget continuation theorem.
The universal assertion that its budget is finite is a separate obligation.
This does not rename the earlier global weak-solution existence statement
called "Weak A", or change the project goal.


13.1 E12 — QFA ON ALL SOLENOIDAL L^2 DATA

Let
  E_A = L^2_sigma(R^3),
  tau_A(a,nu) = [8/(27pi^4)] ||a||_2^4/nu^5.

For every nu>0 and every a in E_A, QFA states:
  (a) LH_nu(a) is nonempty;
  (b) for every u in LH_nu(a) and every delta>tau_A(a,nu),
      the velocity and its normalized pressure are smooth on
      R^3 x [delta,infinity);
  (c) on that tail, for every finite t>=delta,
      ||u(t)||_2^2
        +2nu integral_delta^t ||grad u(r)||_2^2 dr
      =||u(delta)||_2^2;
  (d) the exact trace u(delta) has a unique Leray–Hopf future:
      for every w in LH_nu(u(delta)),
        w(r)=u(delta+r) for every r>=0,
      in the corresponding L^2 representatives.

The strict delta>tau_A presentation matches the canonical earlier QFB
presentation. The safe whole-space cutoff proved here also permits
delta=tau_A. For a=0, tau_A=0 and the energy inequality forces u=0,
so the closed endpoint holds there as well.

Derivation:
E06 uses only the L^2 energy inequality. E07 selects a good H^1
restart time from a set of positive measure. Its argument therefore
does not require H^1 or Schwartz regularity of the original datum.
E05 supplies a global strong continuation from that restart, and
weak–strong uniqueness identifies it with the specified LH solution.
The smooth tail gives its energy equality, and a new weak–strong
comparison from u(delta) gives the stated unique future.

The uniqueness quantifier is attached to the trace of a particular u.
It does not assert that different LH solutions from the original datum
have identical traces at delta. Uniqueness is within the Leray–Hopf
energy class.

STATUS E12: QFA is established at B-tier for every a in L^2_sigma(R^3).
This is the whole-space counterpart of the periodic smooth-tail statement.


13.2 E13 — MATCH THE FULL FOURIER CHARGE

Use the whole-space Fourier convention
  U_hat(t,xi)=integral_R3 exp(-2pi i x.xi)U(t,x)dx,
understood by L^2 extension when U is not integrable.

Define
  Q_A[U](t)
    = integral_R3 |xi|^2 |U_hat(t,xi)|^2 dxi.

Plancherel gives the exact normalization
  Q_A[U](t) = ||grad U(t)||_2^2/(4pi^2).

The previously used periodic charge, for the unit torus with normalized
volume and the corresponding exp(-2pi i k.x) convention, is
  Q_B[u](t)

13.4 E15 — ONE SPECTRAL FORM FOR THE TWO PROGRAMS

Let Omega be either the unit torus T^3 or R^3. Its Fourier parameter
space and measure are respectively:
  Omega=T^3: Z^3 with counting measure;

  Q_Omega[u]
    = integral_{dual Omega} |zeta|^2 |u_hat(zeta)|^2 dmu_Omega(zeta)
    = ||grad u||_2^2/(4pi^2).

For the periodic mean-zero data, the k=0 term is absent (or contributes
zero). On R^3, no mean-zero condition is imposed. In particular, a
whole-space L^2 field need not have a defined spatial integral.

The data spaces are:
  Tail theorem:
    E_B=L^2_{sigma,0}(T^3),
    E_A=L^2_sigma(R^3).
  Classical global target:
    D_B=C^infinity_{sigma,0}(T^3),
    D_A=S_sigma(R^3).

The clocks have a common dependence on datum size and viscosity:
  tau_Omega(a,nu)=C_Omega ||a||_2^4/nu^5,
with the chosen constants
  C_T3=1/(8pi^2),
  C_R3=8/(27pi^4).

These constants belong to different domain estimates and normalizations.
Their numerical comparison is not a comparison of the same initial-data
problem on two domains.

The common theorem structure is:

  WF_Omega:

The A and B labels distinguish the underlying domain and data class.
