This is the cumulative mathematical development of Weak_Fourierman_B. It
supersedes the mathematical stop point in version 0.3.1: that version lacked
a supplied physical continuation theorem and compatible same-path gluing.
Here the actual enstrophy estimate is derived, periodic H1 local theory is
supplied by a primary published theorem, and the comparison/gluing argument
is given explicitly. Those mathematical steps have not thereby been compiled
in Lean. Existing accepted partial build evidence remains unchanged.

The conclusion is conditional on the stated finite Fourier budget. No proof
that every smooth datum satisfies that budget is claimed. The indexed QFB
results are reused as existing theorems, with their concrete physical types;
their complete foundational developments are not reproved in this document.

1. DEFINITIONS AND EXACT PREMISES

Space: the unit torus T3 = R3/Z3 with volume one, represented by unit-periodic
lifts on R3. Spatial integrals below are over the closed unit cube; its
boundary has measure zero. All norms are genuine Lebesgue-space norms.

Fix nu > 0 and a real vector field a satisfying SmoothSigmaZero:
  a is C-infinity, unit-periodic, divergence-free, and has spatial mean zero.
The equation is the actual unforced incompressible Navier-Stokes system

  partial_t u + (u dot grad)u + grad p = nu Delta u,
  div u = 0,       u(0) = a.

Pressure is fixed by spatial mean zero. Classical smoothness at t=0 means
smoothness up to the forward-time boundary, with the right time derivative
there. These are the conventions of SingularClosure.GlobalSmoothNSE.

Let (u^a,p^a) be the unique maximal smooth strong trajectory constructed in
Section 3. Its lifetime Tmax belongs to (0,infinity]. Every finite compact
slab strictly before Tmax has smooth velocity and normalized pressure,
including at t=0. The same trajectory is retained throughout the proof.

For any smooth periodic field v put
  E(v) = integral_cube |v|^2,
  D(v) = integral_cube |grad v|^2
       = sum_j integral_cube |partial_j v|^2.
There is no factor 1/2 in E. Along u^a also put
  Z(t) = integral_cube |Delta u^a(t)|^2.

Use literal Fourier coefficients with the period-one convention:
  u_hat_k(t) = integral_cube u^a(t,x) exp(-2*pi*i*k dot x) dx,
  Q(t) = sum_{k in Z3, k != 0} |k|^2 |u_hat_k(t)|_C3^2,
  |u_hat_k|_C3^2 = sum_{j=1}^3 |u_hat_{k,j}|_C^2.

Define
  Tf(a,nu) = ||a||_L2^4 / (8*pi^2*nu^5),
  tau(a,nu) = min(Tmax(a,nu), Tf(a,nu)).

The minimum is finite because Tf is finite. When Tmax=infinity, tau=Tf;
one never converts infinity to a real before taking this finite minimum.

The additional analytic premise is exactly
  MQ := integral_(0,tau) Q(t)^2 dt < infinity.                 (BUDGET)

The sum and integral here initially mean nonnegative extended sums and
integrals. They retain the value infinity on divergent input. A totalized
real tsum or a totalized real Bochner integral does not encode BUDGET.
Section 4 justifies real summability, measurability and integrability for
the expressions subsequently used. The budget concerns the full infinite
frequency sum, on the whole open interval. Separate finiteness on every
smaller compact interval is not BUDGET.

2. THEOREM AND DEPENDENCY ORDER

Weak_Fourierman_B, B-tier mathematical statement:

  For every nu>0 and every smooth periodic solenoidal mean-zero datum a,
  for its maximal strong trajectory (u^a,p^a),

    integral_(0,min(Tmax,Tf))
      (sum_{k != 0} |k|^2 |u_hat_k^a(t)|_C3^2)^2 dt < infinity

  implies that there exist actual fields U and P with

    SingularClosure.GlobalSmoothNSE nu a U P,

  and, for every 0 <= t < Tmax and every x,
    U(t,x)=u^a(t,x),       P(t,x)=p^a(t,x).

Thus the maximal trajectory has infinite lifetime. The finite Fourier budget
remains the admission hypothesis, not a proved universal bound.

Dependency order:
  periodic physical local theory + uniqueness
    -> unique maximal strong trajectory;
  actual Fourier identities + full squared budget
    -> finite integral D^2 and actual L4_t L6_x;
  actual enstrophy estimate + finite integral D^2
    -> uniform H1 bound up to a possible finite endpoint;
  uniform physical local lifespan + uniqueness
    -> same-path continuation -> Tf < Tmax;
  smooth datum -> actual EnergySigmaZero datum -> Q01 weak existence;
  localized relative energy -> identification with that weak path;
  Q09 after Tf -> actual smooth tail of the same weak path;
  pointwise velocity and normalized-pressure agreement -> smooth gluing;
  zero case + all lifetime cases -> the stated global physical conclusion.

The local-front and homogeneous Fourier-to-Serrin endpoints are alternative
analytic routes. Continuation below uses the stronger direct information
integral D^2 < infinity, so no additional unproved physical Serrin criterion
is inserted into the argument.

3. PHYSICAL LOCAL EXISTENCE, UNIQUENESS AND MAXIMALITY

Classical input [L1]: Terence Tao, Localisation and compactness properties
of the Navier-Stokes global regularity problem, Analysis & PDE 6(1) (2013),
25-107, DOI 10.2140/apde.2013.6.25. Theorem 5.1, printed pp.48-49, gives
periodic H1 local existence, uniqueness, H1 continuity and preservation of
smoothness; Corollary 5.2, p.50, gives maximal development and finite-time
H1 blowup. For zero forcing and viscosity one, a universal c>0 allows
duration S when S*||b||_H1^4 <= c. The pressure convention is normalized
by the mean-zero Poisson solution, equation (9). We use the unit-period,
mean-zero specialization, with smooth divergence-free data.

Publisher source:
  https://msp.org/apde/2013/6-1/apde-v6-n1-p02-s.pdf

The following specialization and composition are our argument. Viscosity
rescaling is
  u(t,x)=nu v(nu*t,x),       p(t,x)=nu^2 q(nu*t,x).
Substitution in the PDE verifies viscosity nu on the left from viscosity
one on the right. For a physical datum b, v(0)=b/nu.

Given an H1 norm bound R, choose
  h(nu,R)=c*nu^3/(nu+R)^4 > 0.
For ||b||_H1 <= R, the viscosity-one duration S=nu*h satisfies
  S*||b/nu||_H1^4 <= c*R^4/(nu+R)^4 <= c.
Hence the physical solution exists for at least h(nu,R), independently of
higher derivatives, any Galerkin cutoff, and the restart time. Smooth b
gives a smooth solution and normalized pressure up to the initial boundary.

Smooth periodic solutions on a compact time slab belong to the H1 mild
class: spatial projection eliminates normalized pressure, and integrating
the heat equation with the actual smooth nonlinearity gives the Duhamel
formula. Consequently [L1] uniqueness applies to the physical solutions
used here. Alternatively, their usual relative-energy argument on each
compact slab proves velocity uniqueness. Subtracting their PDEs then makes
the pressure difference spatially constant; equal zero means fix it to zero.

Periodicity and incompressibility are supplied by the local theory. The
velocity mean remains its initial value: integrate the PDE over the torus;
the divergence-form convection, pressure gradient and Laplacian integrate
to zero. Pressure is normalized at every time. Smoothness ensures the
needed integrability and the same identities at t=0 by right limits.

For completeness, maximality can be assembled without treating a generic
ODE theorem as a PDE theorem. Let A be the positive finite durations admitting
a smooth physical solution from a. Local existence makes A nonempty.
Uniqueness identifies any two such solutions on their common time interval,
including normalized pressure. Let Tmax=sup A in the extended nonnegative
reals. For each t<Tmax choose a member of A whose duration exceeds t and use
its value to define u^a(t),p^a(t). Uniqueness makes this independent of choice.
Every compact slab before Tmax is the restriction of one such solution, so
all PDE, trace, smoothness and normalization properties hold there.

Any solution extending this same path past finite Tmax would yield a duration
in A larger than its supremum. This is the required maximality contradiction.
No global regularity or finite-endpoint continuation is assumed here.

4. FULL PHYSICAL FOURIER BUDGET AND MEASURABILITY

For every smooth periodic mean-zero slice v, its actual Fourier coefficients
decay rapidly. Thus both the unweighted and |k|^2-weighted squared coefficient
series converge. The coefficient at k=0 vanishes. The actual-field Parseval
identities give
  E(v)=sum_{k != 0} |v_hat_k|^2,
  D(v)=4*pi^2 sum_{k != 0} |k|^2 |v_hat_k|^2.               (P)
The same normalization is supplied by the pinned physical Fourier layer.

In particular, for every 0<t<Tmax,
  D(t)=4*pi^2 Q(t),        D(t)^2=16*pi^4 Q(t)^2.
This is an identity for the actual physical trajectory. Truncating its
coefficients is not asserted to produce a Galerkin solution.

Fix any open finite front (0,T) before or up to Tmax. Around each t0 in this
front, choose a compact time neighborhood strictly inside the front. Joint
smoothness gives continuous bounded fields and derivatives on its product
with the compact cube. Parameter integration then makes E(t), D(t) and
integral_cube |u(t)|^6 continuous locally in t. Thus these functions, Q(t),
and the spatial L6 norm are measurable on the entire open front.

There is also a strongly measurable L6-valued representative: local uniform
spatial continuity implies continuity of t -> [u(t)] in genuine L6, since
the L6 difference is bounded by the uniform difference on a volume-one cube.
No endpoint bound or endpoint continuity is inferred from this local argument.

Applying (P) to BUDGET gives genuine integrability and
  MD := integral_(0,tau) D(t)^2 dt = 16*pi^4 MQ < infinity. (F)
For a nonnegative measurable function, finite extended integral is equivalent
to integrability; only after that fact is a real integral used. The Mathlib
adapters are lintegral_ofReal_ne_top_iff_integrable, Integrable.mono' and
integrable_norm_rpow_iff, with the relevant restricted time measure.

5. THE TWO INDEXED FOURIER-TO-SERRIN ENDPOINTS

The arbitrary-slice theorem CRITICAL_SMOOTH_L6_FOURTH_BOUND provides K>0,
independent of the slice, such that
  ||v||_L6^4 <= K*(9*D(v)^2+E(v)^2).

The homogeneous improvement uses actual mean-zero Fourier representatives.
The pinned energy/dissipation Parseval identities and
criticalSobolevLower_norm_le at orders 0 and 2 give
  E(v)=||v0||^2 <= ||v1||^2=D(v).
Equivalently, (P) gives the sharper Poincare bound E<=D/(4*pi^2).
The coarse E<=D is sufficient. It follows that
  ||u(t)||_L6^4 <= 10*K*D(t)^2 = 160*K*pi^4*Q(t)^2.
Measurability from Section 4 and domination by the full budget yield

  integral_(0,tau) ||u(t)||_L6^4 dt <= 160*K*pi^4 MQ < infinity,
  u in L4((0,tau); L6(T3)).                               (S)

If C_H=10*K is named as the homogeneous constant, the coefficient is
16*pi^4*C_H. No extra square of K occurs. This analytic implication needs
joint local smoothness, periodicity and mean zero, but no PDE, initial trace
or energy inequality. Those physical properties are used elsewhere.

The retained inhomogeneous endpoint also remains valid. Pairing the actual
local NSE with u and integrating by parts gives
  E'(t)=-2*nu*D(t).
The convection integral vanishes by incompressibility and periodicity; the
pressure integral vanishes by the same integration-by-parts identity.
Local differentiation under the integral is justified on compact time-cube
products. A strong initial L2 trace implies E(t)->E(a). Thus
  E(t)+2*nu*integral_s^t D=E(s),       0<s<=t<T,
  0<=E(t)<=E(a).
For finite T, the original slice inequality consequently gives
  integral_(0,T) ||u(t)||_L6^4
    <= K*(9*integral_(0,T) D(t)^2+T*E(a)^2).
This retains the earlier finite-front/trace/energy development while (S)
removes the unnecessary bounded-energy term for mean-zero fields.

6. ACTUAL ENSTROPHY ESTIMATE AND FINITE-ENDPOINT CONTINUATION

All calculations below are first on compact slabs [0,b] with b<Tmax. The
local solution is smooth there. Pair the actual NSE with -Delta u. Periodic
integration by parts and div u=0 cancel the pressure term and give
  (1/2)D'(t)+nu*Z(t)
      = integral_cube ((u dot grad)u) dot Delta u.

Holder with exponents (6,3,2), homogeneous periodic Sobolev and interpolation
give
  |integral ((u dot grad)u) dot Delta u|
      <= ||u||_6 ||grad u||_3 ||Delta u||_2
      <= C D^(1/2) D^(1/4) Z^(1/4) Z^(1/2)
       = C D^(3/4) Z^(3/4).
The gradient interpolation follows from L2/L6 interpolation. Each spatial
derivative has mean zero; apply periodic Sobolev to it. Summing the component
estimates and using ||grad^2 u||_2^2=||Delta u||_2^2 gives the displayed bound.
The last identity follows by two periodic integrations by parts, or by
sum_{i,j} k_i^2 k_j^2=|k|^4 in Parseval.

Young's inequality with exponents 4/3 and 4 yields
  C D^(3/4) Z^(3/4) <= (nu/2) Z+C' nu^(-3)D^3.
After multiplying by 2, fix C_* > 0 and obtain the physical inequality
  D'+nu Z <= kappa D^3,       kappa=C_*/nu^3.              (E1)
This derives the actual-field premise needed by the indexed scalar cubic
budget theorem; that scalar theorem alone would not establish (E1).

Suppose 0<T*<infinity is the maximal endpoint and
  M=integral_(0,T*) D^2 < infinity.
For t<T* define A(t)=kappa*integral_0^t D^2. Multiplying (E1) by exp(-A)
and differentiating gives
  (exp(-A)D)' +nu exp(-A)Z <= 0.
Integrate on [0,t] and multiply by exp(A(t)). Since A is nondecreasing,
  D(t)+nu*integral_0^t Z <= D(0) exp(A(t))
                          <= D(0) exp(kappa M).          (E2)
Indeed, the weighted Z integrand after multiplication has the factor
exp(A(t)-A(s))>=1. No division by D is used, including when D(0)=0.

The energy identity gives E(t)<=E(0). Hence, using the physical H1 norm,
  ||u(t)||_H1^2 <= E(0)+D(0) exp(kappa M)=:R^2, t<T*.
A fixed norm-equivalence factor can be absorbed into R for the convention
of [L1]. The bound is uniform all the way to the endpoint from below; no
value or continuity at T* has been assumed.

Section 3 supplies the same positive restart length h(nu,R) for every slice
u(s), s<T*. Choose 0<s<T* so close that T*-s<h/2. Restart from the smooth
slice u(s). The new physical solution exists on [s,s+h], including times
beyond T*. Uniqueness identifies it and its normalized pressure with the
old solution on every compact portion of [s,T*). Therefore they agree on
their whole overlap. Splice strictly inside that overlap: all derivatives
and PDE identities match locally, producing an extension past T*.

This contradicts maximality. We have proved, at B-tier,
  finite Tmax => integral_(0,Tmax) D(t)^2 dt = infinity,
or equivalently by (P), divergence of the full squared Fourier budget at
a finite maximal endpoint. This is a same-trajectory continuation result.

The source-level counterpart still requires physical local H1 theory and
the actual-field enstrophy adapter in Lean. Fixed-cutoff ODE existence,
a continuation certificate assumed as an input, or a globally quantified
regular-solution record is not a substitute for these mathematical steps.

7. STRICT OVERLAP WITH THE EXACT QFB CUTOFF

First assume a!=0. Smoothness and periodicity imply ||a||_L2>0: if the norm
were zero, a would vanish almost everywhere on the cube, then everywhere
by continuity and periodicity. Thus Tf>0, and tau>0 because Tmax>0.

If Tmax were finite and Tmax<=Tf, then tau=Tmax. Formula (F) supplies the
finite endpoint budget used in Section 6, contradicting maximality.
In particular, Tmax=Tf is excluded by continuation, not by a tail theorem
that applies only strictly after Tf. Consequently
  Tf < Tmax.                                               (O)

Choose finite real times
  Tf < delta < s < Tmax.
Such times exist whether Tmax is finite or infinite. The extended-real
formal helper is ENNReal.lt_iff_exists_real_btwn; ordinary intermediate
real times suffice mathematically. There is no requirement Tmax>2*Tf.

8. ACTUAL WEAK DATUM AND ONE FIXED GLOBAL LERAY-HOPF PATH

Let a2=physicalFieldL2(a), the genuine physical L2 equivalence class. The
indexed representative and norm identities give
  a2=a almost everywhere on the cube,       ||a2||_2^2=E(a).
For every smooth periodic scalar test phi, periodic integration by parts
and div a=0 give integral a dot grad phi=0. This is exactly the arbitrary
physical-field cancellation in critical_smooth_integral_inner_gradient_eq_zero.
The pairing transfers to a2 by a.e. equality. Pairing with a coordinate
unit vector gives the corresponding component of integral a=0. Thus
  SmoothSigmaZero(a) -> EnergySigmaZero(a2).

The actual norm identity also identifies the cutoff, without rescaling it:
  cutoff(a2,nu)=||a2||_2^4/(8*pi^2*nu^5)=Tf(a,nu).
The nonzero physical class assertion follows from the same continuity and
periodicity argument as in Section 7, or the indexed Fourier injectivity.

Apply Q01_LERAY_HOPF_EXISTENCE to nu>0 and EnergySigmaZero(a2). It provides
one pair (v,G) satisfying CriticalLerayHopf nu a2 v G. Fix this pair for all
remaining comparisons and tail applications. Q01 supplies actual weak
existence; it is not an additional assumed existence certificate.

In particular, v has the strong initial L2 trace to a2, weak L2 continuity,
the actual weak momentum equation, spatial weak H1 structure almost every
time, and the initial and interval energy inequalities. The interval
inequality holds for almost every starting time and every subsequent time
in each finite slab. These are explicit fields of the indexed predicate.

9. FINITE-FRONT WEAK-STRONG IDENTIFICATION

Fix a finite 0<b<Tmax and choose b<b'<Tmax. Smoothness up to time zero gives
  L_b=sup_{0<=r<=b, x in cube} ||grad u^a(r,x)||_op < infinity.
Let z=v-u^a in physical L2 and H=G-grad u^a. For every admissible energy
starting time 0<s<=t<=b, the relative-energy estimate is

  ||z(t)||_2^2+2*nu*integral_s^t ||H||_2^2
    <= ||z(s)||_2^2
       -2*integral_s^t integral_cube z_i z_j partial_j u_i^a
    <= ||z(s)||_2^2+2*L_b*integral_s^t ||z(r)||_2^2 dr.    (RE)

Here is the localized derivation; no global smooth reference is assumed.
Choose a smooth time cutoff eta, equal to one on [0,b] and zero on a
neighborhood of [b',infinity). Define the positive-time weak test eta*u^a
on its local domain and zero beyond its support. The result is an admissible
smooth periodic solenoidal test. Its extension is used only as a test, never
as an NSE solution outside the front.

On [s,t], the weak momentum identity and the actual equation for u^a yield
  <v(t),u^a(t)>-<v(s),u^a(s)>
    = -2*nu*integral_s^t integral G:grad u^a
      +integral_s^t integral (v_i v_j-v_i u_j^a) partial_j u_i^a.
Weak incompressibility cancels the pressure pairing; weak gradient
integration by parts gives integral v dot Delta u^a=-integral G:grad u^a.
The nonlinear term equals integral z_i z_j partial_j u_i^a because the
remaining integral is z dot grad(|u^a|^2/2)=0 by weak incompressibility.

Add v's interval energy inequality and u^a's energy equality, and subtract
twice the cross identity. Expanding the squared norms gives the first line
of (RE). The operator-norm bound gives its second line. All these integrals
exist: weak velocities are in the finite-time energy class, gradients are
time-L2, and the strong reference and its derivatives are bounded on the
compact slab. No totalized-integral cancellation is used.

Choose admissible s_n decreasing to zero from the full-measure set supplied
by the interval energy inequality. The two strong initial traces imply
||z(s_n)||_2 -> 0. Integral Gronwall applied to (RE) gives, for each t in(0,b],
  ||z(t)||_2^2 <= ||z(s_n)||_2^2 exp(2*L_b*(t-s_n)) -> 0.
This version uses the integral inequality for every t and local integrability;
it does not require assumed strong continuity of v at every time.

At t=0 the same equality is the initial condition. Since b<Tmax is arbitrary,
  for every 0<=t<Tmax, v(t)=u^a(t) almost everywhere on the cube. (ID)

This proves the required localization of weak-strong uniqueness. The existing
comparison theorem whose reference is already global is not applied with an
unsatisfied hypothesis. Compact-front bounded gradients suffice here; a
whole-front Serrin assumption is unnecessary for this identification step.

10. SAME-PATH QFB TAIL AND NORMALIZED-PRESSURE GLUING

For the fixed v,G of Section 8 and delta>Tf, apply the indexed theorem
Q09_POST_CUTOFF_SMOOTHNESS. Its exact conclusion is HasSmoothTail nu v delta:
there exist w,q satisfying ClassicalTail nu delta w q and
  for every t>=delta, v(t)=w(t) almost everywhere on the cube.
The tail has actual joint velocity and pressure smoothness on [delta,infinity),
periodicity, divergence zero, the physical NSE and both zero-mean conditions.
The theorem applies to the supplied weak path v, not an unrelated replacement.

Together with (ID), this gives u^a(t)=w(t) a.e. in space for delta<t<Tmax.
Both spatial fields are continuous and periodic. Equality a.e. implies equality
on the cube interior by continuity, then on the boundary by continuity, and
on all R3 by periodicity. Equivalently, transfer the actual Fourier coefficients
and use datumFourierCoeff_injective_continuous_periodic. Hence u^a=w as joint
functions on the open overlap (delta,Tmax) times R3.

Their actual spacetime derivatives agree there. Subtracting the two NSE
equations yields grad_x(p^a-q)=0. Connectedness of R3 makes the pressure
difference spatially constant at each such time. Its cube mean is zero, so
the constant is zero. Thus p^a=q pointwise on the same open overlap.

Choose s as in Section 7. For t>=0 define
  U(t)=u^a(t), P(t)=p^a(t) if t<=s;
  U(t)=w(t),   P(t)=q(t)   if t>s.
Give these fields arbitrary values at negative times; the physical contract
and all derivatives at t=0 are restricted to the nonnegative half-line.

Choose r>0 with (s-r,s+r) contained in (delta,Tmax). On this neighborhood the
two formulas equal the same smooth pair, so the glued pair is jointly smooth
through the seam. Near zero it equals the original local pair on a full
relative neighborhood of forward spacetime, preserving initial smoothness
and the right time derivative. Away from the seam, smoothness and the PDE
are inherited from the corresponding local pair. This is the mathematical
locality principle behind contDiffOn_of_locally_contDiffOn.

Every required GlobalSmoothNSE field is now obtained:
  velocity_smooth; pressure_smooth;
  velocity_periodic; pressure_periodic;
  divergence_free; equation at every t>=0;
  initial U(0)=a; velocity_mean_zero; pressure_mean_zero.
Smooth periodic fields are integrable on the compact cube, so the explicit
integrability clauses in both mean-zero predicates are also satisfied.

Therefore there exist U,P with SingularClosure.GlobalSmoothNSE nu a U P.
For 0<=t<Tmax they agree with the original trajectory: by definition before
s and by overlap equality after s. The same statement holds for pressure.
If Tmax had been finite, this global pair would extend it, contradicting
maximality. Thus Tmax=infinity as well.

11. ROOT ASSEMBLY AND EDGE CASES

(i) a=0. Then Tf=tau=0, and BUDGET is vacuous. The explicit U=0,P=0 pair
    satisfies GlobalSmoothNSE by globalSmoothNSE_zero. Local strong uniqueness
    identifies the original maximal solution with it on every compact front.
    Normalized pressure is zero; maximality gives Tmax=infinity.

(ii) a!=0 and finite Tmax<=Tf. Then tau=Tmax, (F) supplies the finite endpoint
     enstrophy-square integral, and Section 6 contradicts maximality. This
     includes equality Tmax=Tf.

(iii) a!=0 and Tf<Tmax<infinity. Sections 8-10 identify the weak path, obtain
      its tail, and glue a global smooth extension, again contradicting finite
      maximality. This case does not need a budget beyond Tf.

(iv) Tmax=infinity. Every finite slab of the original pair is smooth with all
     physical properties. Locality gives GlobalSmoothNSE directly on forward
     spacetime. The construction in Section 10 is consistent with the same pair.

All possibilities have been covered. The theorem of Section 2 follows from
BUDGET, the stated datum/viscosity hypotheses, the indexed Q01/Q09 theorems,
and the classical local theory [L1]. No continuation or regularity certificate
has been added as a premise, and no universal finiteness claim has been made.
