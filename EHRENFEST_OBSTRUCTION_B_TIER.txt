2. DEFINITIONS AND EXPLICIT PREMISES

Domain: the volume-one torus T^3 = R^3/Z^3; viscosity nu > 0; zero forcing.
Cutoffs in this workstream satisfy N>=1. The zero-dimensional N=0 cutoff
is omitted; it is irrelevant to every eventual-N hypothesis and conclusion.
D_inf := real C^infinity divergence-free mean-zero vector fields on T^3.
NSE_B(nu) := for every a in D_inf, there exist U,P with GlobalSmoothNSE nu a U P.
This is the project's universal smooth periodic existence statement.

P_N is the orthogonal Fourier cutoff to nonzero frequencies |k| <= N,
including both k and -k and commuting with the Leray projector P and A=-Delta.
V_N is its finite-dimensional REAL solenoidal phase space. It is not the
unrestricted space of complex coefficient arrays. Use the existing reality
and solenoidality invariant-state interface and then a real orthonormal basis.

B_N(v,w) := P_N P((v dot grad)w), for v,w in V_N.
b_N(q) := -nu A_N q - B_N(q,q), A_N := A restricted to V_N.
Phi_N^t(q) is the exact ODE flow generated by b_N.
u_N(t) := Phi_N^t(P_N a), with ONE datum a independent of N.

Fluid quantities (do not confuse these with phase-space wavefunctions):
  E_N(q) := ||q||_L2^2;
  D_N(q) := ||grad q||_L2^2;
  e_N(q) := D_N(q)/2;
  Z_N(q) := ||A_N q||_L2^2;
  W_N(q) := -<B_N(q,q),A_N q>_L2.
Equivalently W_N = integral omega dot S(q) omega, with omega=curl q and
S(q)=(grad q + transpose(grad q))/2.
The symbol e_N here is half the squared gradient norm, not the critical H^(1/2) energy used in
the earlier signed-production discussion.

Fix e_* > 0 and define the bounded reciprocal observable
  Vrec_N(q) := (1 + e_N(q)/e_*)^(-1),   0 < Vrec_N(q) <= 1.
Define its directional derivative
  c_N(q) := D Vrec_N(q)[b_N(q)].

Phase-space Hilbert space:
  Hspace_N := L2(V_N, Lebesgue dq; C).
  Dcore_N := C_c^infinity(V_N; C).
  M_g is multiplication by g on its natural maximal L2 domain.
For hbar > 0 define on Dcore_N
  H0_N := -i hbar (b_N dot grad_q + (div_q b_N)/2).
H_N denotes the self-adjoint closure constructed in E04, not an assumed
everywhere-defined bounded operator.

For thresholds R_N > 0 define
  tau_N := inf {t >= 0 : e_N(u_N(t)) >= R_N},
where inf(empty)=+infinity. Let T_gamma := 1/gamma.

The proposed assumption is a Prop/hypothesis, not a global Lean axiom:

StoppedCommutatorBound(nu) :=
  exists a in D_inf, e_*>0, gamma>0, (R_N)>0, N0,
    R_N -> +infinity as N->infinity, and
    for every N>=N0 and every real t with
      0 <= t < min(tau_N,T_gamma),
        c_N(u_N(t)) <= -gamma.

All trajectories in this condition are the canonical exact Galerkin flows
of P_N a. Neither the datum nor gamma nor e_* may vary with N. Finite-N
solutions are global; the condition stops when a finite threshold is hit.
For sufficiently large N the thresholds exceed the bounded initial enstrophy.
The zero datum cannot satisfy the condition because c_N(0)=0.

Formalization may avoid extended-real hitting times using the equivalent
pre-hit guard: t<T_gamma and e_N(u_N(s))<R_N for every 0<=s<=t.
Continuity supplies equivalence with t<tau_N. Keep the guard explicit.

4. COMPLETE MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION

E01. Physical finite-state algebra.
Reality c(-k)=conj(c(k)), k dot c(k)=0 and c(0)=0 identify a real vector
subspace of the finite Fourier arrays. P_N and P are orthogonal and commute
with derivatives. The polynomial b_N preserves this subspace. On V_N,
  <B_N(q,q),q> = integral (q dot grad)q dot q
               = (1/2) integral q dot grad |q|^2 = 0
by periodic integration by parts and div q=0. A_N is positive self-adjoint.
The finite-dimensional polynomial RHS is C^infinity and locally Lipschitz.
These are statements about the actual NSE coefficient vector field.

E02. Complete flow in both time directions.
Along a local solution,
  (d/dt)||q||^2 = -2 nu <A_N q,q>.
Thus forward energy is nonincreasing. If lambda_max(N) is the largest
eigenvalue of A_N, the time-reversed solution r(s)=q(-s) satisfies
  (d/ds)||r||^2 <= 2 nu lambda_max(N)||r||^2,
and ||r(s)||^2 <= exp(2 nu lambda_max(N)s)||r(0)||^2.
No finite-time escape is possible in either direction in this finite-
dimensional locally Lipschitz ODE. Continuation, uniqueness, and smooth
dependence therefore give a complete smooth flow. The group law and inverse
follow from uniqueness. D Phi_N^t is invertible with inverse D Phi_N^(-t)
at the transported point. No bound uniform in N follows from this argument.

E03. Half-density representation.
Define U_N by the formula in E03. Change variables q=Phi_N^t(p): the
Jacobian factors cancel, giving ||U_N(t)f||_2=||f||_2. The flow law and
Jacobian chain rule give U_N(t+s)=U_N(t)U_N(s), U_N(0)=I, and inverse U_N(-t).
For f in Dcore_N, smooth dependence and a common compact support for |t|<=S
give L2 continuity by dominated convergence. Density of Dcore_N and the
isometry extend strong continuity to every f in Hspace_N. Composition with
a smooth diffeomorphism and multiplication by its smooth positive Jacobian
factor preserve Dcore_N. Fluid energy may dissipate while this phase-space
transport is unitary: these are distinct quantities.

E04. Generator and domain proof.
For f in Dcore_N, differentiation in L2 at t=0 gives
  d U_N(t)f/dt|_0 = -b_N dot grad f -(div b_N)f/2 =: L0_N f.
Compact-support integration by parts shows L0_N is skew-symmetric, so
H0_N=i hbar L0_N is symmetric. Stone's unitary-group theorem provides a
skew-adjoint generator L_N extending L0_N and a self-adjoint H_N=i hbar L_N.
It remains to prove that the specified core, not merely some extension, is
correct. Here is a core proof rather than an assumption of self-adjointness.

For chi in C_c^infinity(R) set S_chi f = integral chi(t)U_N(t)f dt.
Then S_chi f is in D(L_N) and L_N S_chi f=-integral chi'(t)U_N(t)f dt.
Approximate f in L2 by f_j in Dcore_N. Both integrals show convergence of
S_chi f_j to S_chi f in the graph norm of L_N. Moreover S_chi f_j is smooth
with compact support: the union of flow images of supp(f_j) over the compact
support of chi is compact, and every derivative may be passed under the
integral on that set. Thus S_chi f_j belongs to Dcore_N.
For f in D(L_N), choose an approximate identity chi_epsilon. Strong
continuity of U_N(t) on both f and L_N f gives S_chi_epsilon f -> f in graph
norm. Diagonal approximation proves Dcore_N is a generator core.
Consequently closure(H0_N)=H_N is self-adjoint and U_N=exp(-it H_N/hbar).
No bounded/everywhere-defined symmetric-map theorem substitutes for this
unbounded-domain argument. A suitable formal Stone/core infrastructure has
not been identified as an already accepted Methboke endpoint.

E05. Exact Heisenberg and Ehrenfest identities.
Let g be real and smooth. For f in Dcore_N the product rule yields
  H0_N(g f)-g H0_N(f) = -i hbar Dg[b_N] f.
The half-divergence terms cancel. This proves the commutator identity.
Direct substitution into U_N(-t)M_g U_N(t) gives multiplication by
g(Phi_N^t(q)). Change of variables identifies the natural maximal domains,
so the conjugation equality is an operator equality with domains, not only
a formal symbolic expression. For f in Dcore_N its transported support
stays compact on finite time intervals; differentiation of the expectation
is justified there and gives the Ehrenfest derivative.

For exact evaluation at q0, choose L2-normalized smooth packets f_epsilon
supported in the epsilon-ball around q0. For any smooth g,
  <U_N(t)f_epsilon,M_g U_N(t)f_epsilon>
    = integral |f_epsilon(q)|^2 g(Phi_N^t(q))dq
    -> g(Phi_N^t(q0)),
uniformly on compact time intervals, by continuity on a compact set. The
same applies to Dg[b_N]. Localization is performed at fixed N; no uniform
packet estimate in N or exchange of the two limits is asserted. In the root
proof the commutator multiplier can simply be evaluated on the exact orbit.
Construct packets by scaling a nonzero smooth compact bump and dividing by
its L2 norm. A smooth probability density with integral one is not already
an L2-normalized wavefunction; no unjustified smooth square root is used.

E06. Enstrophy and reciprocal multiplier.
Using orthogonality and commutation of P_N,P,A,
  d e_N(u_N)/dt = <A_N u_N,b_N(u_N)> = W_N(u_N)-nu Z_N(u_N).
The standard periodic vorticity identity also gives W_N=integral omega S omega.
Differentiating the reciprocal with strictly positive denominator gives
  c_N(q) = -(W_N(q)-nu Z_N(q))/(e_*(1+e_N(q)/e_*)^2).
Thus c_N(u_N)<=-gamma is equivalent to
  W_N(u_N)-nu Z_N(u_N) >= gamma e_*(1+e_N(u_N)/e_*)^2.
The sign and viscosity term are indispensable. Robertson uncertainty gives
a magnitude inequality and does not prove this signed dynamical lower bound.
At each fixed N, c_N is bounded: its numerator is polynomial of degree at
most three, whereas its denominator grows quartically on the nonzero-mode
real phase space, where e_N is positive definite. Both Vrec_N and c_N are
smooth everywhere. The bounded core commutator extends to D(H_N): for a
graph-approximating sequence f_j in Dcore_N,
  H_N M_Vrec_N f_j = M_Vrec_N H_N f_j - i hbar M_c_N f_j
has an L2 limit, and closedness proves M_Vrec_N f is in D(H_N) with the same
identity. Thus Ehrenfest's identity is also valid for normalized states in
D(H_N). These boundedness constants may depend on N.

E07. Scalar stopped comparison.
Let 0<r<v(0)<=1, gamma>0, and T=1/gamma. Assume v is continuous on [0,T]
and differentiable before the first time v<=r, with v'<=-gamma there.
Set tbar=(v(0)-r)/gamma. Then 0<tbar<T. If v>r throughout [0,tbar], the
mean-value/derivative-comparison theorem applied to v(t)+gamma*t gives
  v(tbar)<=v(0)-gamma*tbar=r,
a contradiction. Hence some t<=tbar has v(t)<=r. Continuity gives a first
such time with equality. This argument neither presupposes the hitting time
is finite nor continues the drift after the first hit.

E08. Apply the explicit premise and pass only the cutoff to infinity.
For each sufficiently large N, e_N(P_Na)<=||grad a||^2/2<R_N. Put
  r_N=(1+R_N/e_*)^(-1), v_N(t)=Vrec_N(u_N(t)).
The scalar derivative is c_N(u_N(t)). E07 supplies t_N with
  0<=t_N<=(Vrec_N(P_Na)-r_N)/gamma<T_gamma,
  e_N(u_N(t_N))=R_N.
Therefore sup_[0,T_gamma] e_N(u_N(t))>=R_N -> +infinity.
Every individual trajectory is smooth and finite. It is the family of
thresholds, at one fixed initial datum, that diverges. The stopping clause
prevents an impossible demand for forever-negative drift of a positive
bounded observable on a fixed finite-dimensional trajectory.

E09. Actual smooth solution implies uniform Galerkin H1 bounds.
Assume an actual smooth solution U of unforced projected NSE on [0,T] with
U(0)=a. Choose K>=1+sup_[0,T]||U||_H4, finite by smoothness and compactness.
Define v_N=P_N U, r_N=U-v_N, and w_N=u_N-v_N. In this paragraph r_N is a
Fourier tail, unrelated to the scalar r_N in E08. Spectral truncation gives
  sup_t ||r_N||_H3 <= C K/N,
  sup_t ||v_N||_H3 <= K.
Projection of NSE and subtraction from the Galerkin equation give
  w_N' + nu A w_N
    + P_N P(B(w_N,v_N)+B(v_N,w_N)+B(w_N,w_N)) = f_N,
  f_N=P_N P(B(U,U)-B(v_N,v_N)),   w_N(0)=0,
where B(x,y)=(x dot grad)y inside this display before the Leray projection.
The H2 algebra/product estimate on T^3 implies
  ||B(x,y)||_H2 <= C ||x||_H2 ||y||_H3.
Expanding f_N as B(r_N,U)+B(v_N,r_N) therefore yields
  sup_t ||f_N||_H2 <= C K^2/N.
All cutoffs and Leray projections have Sobolev operator norm at most one.

Set Y_N=||w_N||_H2^2. Pair the equation in H2 with w_N. The mixed terms
are bounded by C K Y_N and C K sqrt(Y_N)||w_N||_H3; the quadratic error
term is bounded by C Y_N||w_N||_H3. Young's inequality absorbs the H3
terms into viscosity. Mean zero and the torus spectral gap identify
<A w_N,w_N>_H2 with a coercive H3 quantity. The residual is bounded by
sqrt(Y_N)||f_N||_H2 <= (Y_N+||f_N||_H2^2)/2. Thus
  Y_N' <= C_(nu,K)(Y_N+Y_N^2) + C_(nu,K)/N^2,
with constants independent of N and Y_N(0)=0.

Until the first exit Y_N=1, this gives
  Y_N' <= 2 C_(nu,K)Y_N + C_(nu,K)/N^2,
so Gronwall gives Y_N(t)<=C_(nu,K,T)/N^2. For all sufficiently large N,
the bound is strictly smaller than 1 on [0,T], excluding a first exit by
continuity. The estimate therefore holds throughout [0,T]. In particular,
  sup_t ||u_N(t)||_H1 <= K+1
for all sufficiently large N. Finitely many remaining N are bounded on the
compact time interval by their global smooth ODE flows. This proves the
required uniform bound. Neither the existence of a uniform Galerkin bound
nor a uniform Galerkin H2-integral budget was assumed.

E10. Assemble the conditional root.
Suppose StoppedCommutatorBound(nu), and take its fixed datum a and gamma.
If NSE_B(nu), choose its actual global smooth solution with that same a.
Restrict it to [0,T_gamma]. E09 gives a common finite Galerkin enstrophy
bound there. E08 contradicts that bound because R_N->infinity. Therefore
not NSE_B(nu). In fact this particular a admits no smooth solution through
T_gamma. The already accepted local/maximal theory then locates its maximal
smooth lifespan in (0,T_gamma]. This last localization is a consequence,
not an additional assumption.

10. LITERAL CANONICAL INTERFACE AND CLOSED-SLAB REFINEMENTS

Use a subtype SmoothDatum of the actual Field with SmoothSigmaZero proof.
For every logical index n use the exact physical cutoff n+1:
  canonicalGalerkinSolution nu hnu a.val a.property (n+1).
One nu, hnu, a, eStar, gamma and threshold family R are fixed before n.
The cofinal n+1 reindexing does not change the eventual-positive-cutoff claim.

Let P denote coefficientEnstrophyNonlinearPower (not the Leray projection
in this paragraph). The exact native identity D'=-2*nu*Z-2*P yields
  e=D/2, e'=-nu*Z-P, W=-P,
  V'=(nu*Z+P)/(eStar*(1+e/eStar)^2)=c.
