2. JOINT STRONG CONTINUITY OF THE HEAT ACTION

For elapsed times r,s>=0 and actual L2 fields v,w, linearity and heat
contraction give

  ||S(r)v-S(s)w|| <= ||v-w|| + ||S(r)w-S(s)w||.

The first term tends to zero by continuity of the input field. The second
tends to zero by the accepted strong continuity for the fixed input w.
The norm limit criterion therefore proves continuity of (r,v)|->S(r)v,
including r=0. This is joint strong continuity; no operator-norm
continuity at zero is used.

For each fixed t, the map s|->((t-s).toNNReal,f(s)) is a.e. strongly
measurable under the forcing measure. Its composition with the proved

measurability theorem is also stated for an arbitrary real-time measure.

3. ACTUAL INTEGRABILITY BEFORE USING THE RESPONSE

5. EXACT LOW-FREQUENCY ESTIMATE

The Bochner norm bound and contraction give

  ||K_nu f(t)|| <= integral_0^t ||f(s)||ds.

Holder with exponents two and two, applied to the constant one function
and the scalar norm of f, gives

  ||K_nu f(t)||
    <= sqrt(t) * sqrt(integral_0^t ||f(s)||^2 ds)
    <= sqrt(T) * sqrt(integral_0^T ||f(s)||^2 ds).

The constant is exactly one. There is no viscosity loss and no spatial
Poincare or spectral-gap assumption. The inhomogeneous L2 part is retained
because it is required on the whole space.

product and its a.e. coeFn_toLp equality establish

  ||K_nu f(t)|| <= sqrt(T) * ||hf.toLp(f)||.

This uses the norm of the constructed forcing Lp class, not a separately
supplied numerical bound. It holds at every time of the finite window.

6. CONTINUITY WITH THE MOVING UPPER LIMIT

On the fixed forcing window (0,T], define

  H(t,s)=1_(s<=t) * S((t-s).toNNReal)f(s).

For each t this is a.e. strongly measurable in s and is bounded in norm
by the integrable function ||f(s)||. For fixed s, the map t|->H(t,s) is
continuous at every t other than s: on one side it is zero, and on the
other side it is the strongly continuous heat action on the fixed field
f(s). The exceptional singleton has zero restricted Lebesgue measure.

interval-indicator identity identifies this integral with K_nu f(t).
