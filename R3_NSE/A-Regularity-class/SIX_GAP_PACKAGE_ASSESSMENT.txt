5. G04: WHY TERMINAL CONTROL MUST REMAIN AN EXPLICIT PDE LEMMA

Mere joint smoothness on R3 x [0,T] plus uniform L2 energy does not, by
functional analysis alone, imply a uniform global H1 norm. This can be seen
without asserting any NSE counterexample:

Choose a nonzero smooth compactly supported divergence-free F on R3 with
||F||_2=1 and support in the unit ball. For n>=1 set
  x_n=(n,0,0), F_n(x)=n^(3/2) F(n(x-x_n)).
Then ||F_n||_2=1 and ||grad F_n||_2=n||grad F||_2.
Take t_n=1-2^(-n), pairwise disjoint time bumps beta_n centered at t_n,
supported in (t_n-2^(-n-3),t_n+2^(-n-3)), with beta_n(t_n)=1 and
0<=beta_n<=1. Define U(t,x)=sum_n beta_n(t)F_n(x), extending by zero
outside the bump intervals and for t>=1. This sum is locally finite in
space, including in every neighborhood of each finite point at t=1.
Thus U is jointly smooth and divergence-free, sup_t||U(t)||_2<=1, yet
||grad U(t_n)||_2=n||grad F||_2 -> infinity.

This is a general field example, not an NSE solution. It proves that a
literal competitor's terminal H1 control must use the PDE and its global
spatial estimates, not only the wording smooth plus finite energy.
Agreement with a canonical trajectory on every shorter slab does not supply
this missing uniform estimate by itself.
