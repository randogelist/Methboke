1. Pointwise finite-six algebra.
For q:Fin 6 -> R with all q_i>=0,
  sum_i q_i^2 <= (sum_i q_i)^2.
The cross terms in the square are nonnegative. Mathlib anchor:
  Finset.sum_sq_le_sq_sum_of_nonneg.
For arbitrary real q, finite Cauchy-Schwarz applied to (1,...,1) and q gives
  (sum_i q_i)^2 <= (sum_i 1^2)(sum_i q_i^2)=6 sum_i q_i^2.
Anchor: Finset.sum_mul_sq_le_sq_mul_sq.
The lower bound uses the nonnegative-charge hypothesis. The upper bound
does not require it.

2. Actual extended budgets for an arbitrary measure.
For a measurable carrier A, measure mu, and q:Fin 6 -> A -> R define
  J6(q,mu)=lintegral ofReal(sum_i q_i(t)^2) dmu,
  J(q,mu)=lintegral ofReal((sum_i q_i(t))^2) dmu.
No conversion to real is made in either definition.
If all six charges are nonnegative mu-a.e., monotonicity and step 1 give
  J6 <= J <= 6 J6.
The constant six is finite, so Mathlib lintegral_const_mul' moves it
outside the integral without a measurability premise on the integrand.
The bounds therefore also hold for infinite budgets.

3. Full-budget finiteness equivalence.
From step 2,
  J6 != infinity <-> J != infinity.
Right-to-left uses J6<=J. Left-to-right uses J<=6 J6 and finite constant
multiplication. There is no replacement of the full measure by separate
compact-interval finiteness.

4. Measurable charge splitting.
If each real charge q_i is ae measurable, its squared ofReal integrand is
ae measurable. ofReal preserves the finite sum of nonnegative squares and
lintegral_finsetSum' then yields
  J6 = sum_i lintegral ofReal(q_i(t)^2) dmu.
Hence J6 finite iff each of the six individual full budgets is finite.
Measurability is used for this exact splitting identity, not imposed on the
bounds in step 2 where it is unnecessary.

5. Supplied same-path charge additivity.
If a real scalar Y obeys Y(t)=sum_i q_i(t) mu-a.e., then
  J(q,mu)=lintegral ofReal(Y(t)^2) dmu.
This is an explicit hypothesis of the scalar adapter. In the NSE application
it must later be proved from the actual six orthogonal physical projectors
and the actual weak-gradient/Fourier identities; it is not a new axiom or
an established physical projection theorem here.

6. Full stopped window.
Set mu=volume.restrict (Ioo 0 b). The identity in step 5 identifies J with
ACBudget.stoppedSquareBudget Y b. Thus
  J6 <= stoppedSquareBudget Y b <= 6 J6,
and the two full budgets are finite exactly together. The zero tuple and
the zero window have zero budget.

7. Quantitative connection to the AC cubic bound.
Suppose kappa>=0, Y(0)>=0, Y is AC on every [0,R], 0<=R<b, and
Y'<=kappa Y^3 almost everywhere on (0,b). Under the nonnegative-charge
and additivity inputs above, finite J6 implies finite J. Only then use
monotonicity of toReal to get

The ACBudget scalar theorem gives the SAME bound for all t in [0,b):
  Y(t) <= Y(0) exp(kappa * (6 J6.toReal)).
This is a scalar composition with the complete terminal budget; no local
