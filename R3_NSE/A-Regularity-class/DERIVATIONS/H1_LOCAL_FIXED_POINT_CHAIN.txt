0. CLOSED B-TIER TARGET

For every viscosity nu>0 and real divergence-free datum
  a in H1(R3;R3),
write M=||a||_2 and H=||grad a||_2. If H>0, define

  S0 = 4/sqrt(3),
  C0 = S0^(3/2),
  c0 = 1/(4096 S0^6) = 27/2^24,
  T0(a,nu) = c0 nu^3/H^4.                         (0.1)

The construction below gives an actual unforced NSE velocity and normalized
pressure on [0,T0], with

  u in C([0,T0];H1_sigma(R3)) intersect L2(0,T0;H2(R3)),
  partial_t u in L2(0,T0;L2_sigma(R3)),
  u(0)=a strongly in H1,
  partial_t u - nu Delta u + (u.grad)u + grad p = 0,
  div u=0,

where the equation holds in L2(0,T0;L2) and hence in distributions.
Velocity uniqueness holds throughout this strong class, including among
solutions not initially known to lie in the particular contraction ball.
The canonical pressure is in C([0,T0];L2); pressure gradients are unique.

The local solutions patch uniquely to a maximal development [0,Tmax), and
  Tmax<infinity ==> sup_(t<Tmax)||grad u(t)||_2=infinity.
In particular bounded full H1 norm gives continuation.

The explicit constant is sufficient, with substantial slack; no optimality
claim is made. This local construction uses the admissible nonsharp S0.
The registered sharp constant S6 and numerical cutoff tau_A are unchanged.

If H=0, Plancherel and L2 membership imply a=0: its Fourier transform is
supported on the null set {0}. The zero solution then exists for all times.
No positive time formula is evaluated by dividing by H in this case.

1. PHYSICAL CARRIERS AND NORMALIZATION

All spatial Lp norms use full Lebesgue measure on R3. Vector and tensor
fibers have Euclidean and Frobenius norms, respectively. H1 and H2 mean
actual weak derivatives in L2. H_sigma is the closed L2 subspace satisfying
distributional divergence zero. No zero-average premise is imposed on R3.

Use the L2-isometric extension of
  Ff(xi)=integral_R3 exp(-2 pi i x.xi) f(x) dx.
Put k=2 pi xi and lambda(xi)=|k|^2. Then
  ||grad v||_2^2 = integral lambda |Fv|^2,
  ||Delta v||_2^2 = integral lambda^2 |Fv|^2,
  ||D2 v||_2^2 = sum_(i,j)||partial_i partial_j v||_2^2
                = integral (sum_i k_i^2)^2 |Fv|^2
                = ||Delta v||_2^2.                         (1.1)
The mixed second derivatives are counted with all ordered pairs, as in the
Frobenius Hessian. Equation (1.1) carries no dimension factor.

For xi!=0 let
  P(xi)=I - xi tensor xi/|xi|^2,
and choose P(0)=I. This value has no effect on the L2 operator. The symbol
is a real symmetric orthogonal projection with operator norm<=1, is even,
and commutes with scalar multipliers. Thus its Fourier multiplier P:
  - is an L2 contraction and an orthogonal projection;
  - preserves the Hermitian symmetry characterizing real fields;
  - has image exactly H_sigma;
  - commutes with the heat semigroup and weak spatial derivatives;
  - annihilates any L2 vector field that is a distributional gradient.
These statements follow pointwise from the symbol and then by Plancherel.
The divergence characterization uses the actual weak-derivative Fourier
identity; it is an explicit native formalization requirement below.

The physical H1/H2 graph spaces are complete. Passing to limits in compact
test identities proves the derivative graphs closed in finite products of
L2. The solenoidal subspaces are closed kernels. These facts also identify
the corresponding Fourier graph domains without a Poincare inequality.

2. AN EXPLICIT ADMISSIBLE NONSHARP SOBOLEV CONSTANT

This section proves the constant used in (0.1), so it is not an unspecified
spatial estimate inserted as an assumption.

2.1 Scalar W^(1,1) estimate.
Let g be a smooth compactly supported real scalar function. For i=1,2,3 put
  A_i(x_hat_i)=integral_R |partial_i g(x)| dx_i.
The one-dimensional fundamental theorem gives |g(x)|<=A_i(x_hat_i).
Therefore
  |g(x)|^(3/2) <= sqrt(A_1(x2,x3) A_2(x1,x3) A_3(x1,x2)).

For completeness, the resulting product integral estimate follows from two
Cauchy-Schwarz applications. First integrate in x1:
  integral sqrt(A_2(x1,x3) A_3(x1,x2)) dx1
  <= sqrt(B_2(x3) B_3(x2)),
where B_2=integral A_2 dx1 and B_3=integral A_3 dx1. Then apply
Cauchy-Schwarz on (x2,x3) to sqrt(A_1) sqrt(B_2 B_3). Tonelli yields
  integral_R3 |g|^(3/2)
  <= product_i ||partial_i g||_1^(1/2).
Taking the power 2/3, arithmetic-geometric mean and pointwise
|partial_1g|+|partial_2g|+|partial_3g|<=sqrt(3)|grad g| give
  ||g||_(3/2) <= product_i ||partial_i g||_1^(1/3)
              <= (1/sqrt(3))||grad g||_1.                (2.1)

2.2 Vector/tensor L6 estimate.
For a smooth compactly supported field F with any finite-dimensional real
Euclidean fiber, apply (2.1) to g=|F|^4. This is a smooth polynomial in the
components, and |grad g|<=4|F|^3|grad F|. Holder gives
  ||F||_6^4 <= (4/sqrt(3))||F||_6^3||grad F||_2.
If ||F||_6=0 the desired estimate is immediate; otherwise cancel its cube:
  ||F||_6 <= S0||grad F||_2,  S0=4/sqrt(3).              (2.2)

2.3 Extension to physical H1.
Take standard compact cutoffs chi_R with chi_R=1 on B_R and
|grad chi_R|<=C/R. For F in physical H1, chi_R F -> F in H1: velocity and
gradient tails vanish by L2 integrability, and
  ||F grad chi_R||_2 <= (C/R)||F||_2 -> 0.
Mollifying the cutoff fields supplies smooth compactly supported H1
approximants F_n. Applying (2.2) to F_n-F_m makes them Cauchy in L6.
Their L2 limit and L6 limit agree by an a.e.-convergent subsequence.
Thus (2.2) holds on the actual physical H1 carrier with the same S0.
The proof applies to vector and matrix fibers with no extra fiber constant.

Consequently, for v in H2,
  ||grad v||_6 <= S0||D2v||_2 = S0||Delta v||_2.          (2.3)
Interpolation by Holder also gives
  ||grad v||_3 <= ||grad v||_2^(1/2)||grad v||_6^(1/2).   (2.4)

All powers here concern nonnegative real norms. The proof uses coordinate
FTC, Tonelli, Cauchy-Schwarz, Holder, cutoff/mollification density and
Plancherel. No sharp Sobolev theorem is required for this local IVP.

3. HEAT AND FORCED-HEAT ESTIMATES WITH CONSTANTS

For finite T>0 set
  X_T(u) = max{sup_(0<=t<=T)||grad u(t)||_2,
                sqrt(nu)||Delta u||_(L2(0,T;L2))}.        (3.1)
This controls only homogeneous derivatives. We use it as an estimate, and
perform the fixed point in a separately complete inhomogeneous forcing
space; no completeness claim for arbitrary fields under (3.1) is needed.

3.1 Free heat evolution.
Let A(t)=exp(nu t Delta)a. Its Fourier multiplier is exp(-nu t lambda).
Dominated convergence gives A in C([0,T];H1), A(0)=a, and
  sup_t ||A(t)||_2 <= M,
  sup_t ||grad A(t)||_2 <= H,
  nu integral_0^T ||Delta A||_2^2 dt
    = (1/2) integral lambda(1-exp(-2nu T lambda))|Fa|^2
    <= H^2/2.                                         (3.2)
Hence X_T(A)<=H. The time derivative is nu Delta A in L2_t L2_x.

3.2 Forced heat response.
For f in L2(0,T;L2), define the Bochner Duhamel integral
  K_nu f(t)=integral_0^t exp(nu(t-s)Delta)f(s) ds.        (3.3)
Heat contraction and Cauchy-Schwarz in time give the indispensable
low-frequency estimate
  sup_t ||K_nu f(t)||_2 <= sqrt(T)||f||_(L2_t L2_x).      (3.4)

Initially for smooth spectral/time approximants w=K_nu f, the heat equation
w_t-nu Delta w=f, w(0)=0, yields
  (1/2)d/dt||grad w||_2^2+nu||Delta w||_2^2
    = -<f,Delta w>
    <= (1/(2nu))||f||_2^2+(nu/2)||Delta w||_2^2.
Therefore for every 0<=r<=T,
  ||grad w(r)||_2^2 + nu integral_0^r ||Delta w||_2^2
     <= nu^(-1) integral_0^r ||f||_2^2.                 (3.5)
In particular
  X_T(K_nu f) <= nu^(-1/2)||f||_(L2_t L2_x).             (3.6)
The maximum in (3.1) is essential: (3.5) bounds each term by the same
quantity, without asserting that the supremum term plus the full integral
is bounded by that quantity at a common maximizing time.

Approximate f in L2_t L2_x. Equations (3.4)--(3.6) make the responses
Cauchy in C_t L2_x, C_t dot-H1_x, and L2_t dot-H2_x. Thus the limit is
in C_t H1_x, vanishes strongly in H1 at t=0, and has the claimed Laplacian.
Closed weak-derivative graphs and (1.1) identify the physical derivatives.
The equation gives w_t=nu Delta w+f in L2_t L2_x, with the sufficient bound
  ||w_t||_(L2_t L2_x) <= 2||f||_(L2_t L2_x).             (3.7)
The inhomogeneous H2 membership follows from (3.4), the first-derivative
bound, and (1.1) on the finite interval. Fourier or smooth approximant
arguments justify the limiting Duhamel equation.
Real and solenoidal input f gives real and solenoidal w because the
multiplier is real/even and commutes with P.

4. THE ACTUAL BILINEAR MAP

For u,v in C_t H1 intersect L2_t H2 define
  B(u,v)=P((u.grad)v).
For almost every t, pointwise contraction, Holder and (2.2)--(2.4) give
  ||B(u,v)||_2
  <= ||u||_6||grad v||_3
  <= S0^(3/2)||grad u||_2 ||grad v||_2^(1/2)
                                   ||Delta v||_2^(1/2).
Square and integrate, using Cauchy-Schwarz in time:
  ||B(u,v)||_(L2_tL2_x)^2
  <= S0^3 (sup||grad u||_2)^2(sup||grad v||_2)
                            sqrt(T)||Delta v||_(L2_tL2_x)
  <= S0^3 T^(1/2)nu^(-1/2)X_T(u)^2X_T(v)^2.
Hence
  ||B(u,v)||_(L2_tL2_x)
    <= C0 T^(1/4)nu^(-1/4)X_T(u)X_T(v),
  C0=S0^(3/2).                                         (4.1)

The actual product is Bochner measurable: H1 and H2 representatives are
strongly measurable, and the displayed continuous Sobolev/product maps
preserve strong measurability. It takes values in H_sigma after applying P.
No nonlinear-map estimate is assumed as an opaque port.

5. COMPLETE FORCING SPACE AND BANACH FIXED POINT

Let
  G_T=L2((0,T);H_sigma),
with its actual Bochner L2 norm. This is a complete real Hilbert space,
since H_sigma is a closed subspace of physical L2. For g in G_T define
  u_g=A+K_nu(sqrt(nu)g),
  Psi(g)=-nu^(-1/2) B(u_g,u_g).                         (5.1)
These are actual constructed functions, not supplied solution fields.

Equations (3.2)--(3.6) give
  X_T(u_g)<=H+||g||_G,
  X_T(u_g-u_h)<=||g-h||_G,                              (5.2)
  sup_t||u_g(t)||_2<=M+sqrt(nu T)||g||_G.                (5.3)
Thus low frequencies and the full H1 trace are controlled independently of
the homogeneous estimate. The contraction space has no missing L2 norm.

Put eta=C0 nu^(-3/4)T^(1/4). On the closed ball ||g||_G<=H,
  ||Psi(g)||_G <= eta(2H)^2,
  ||Psi(g)-Psi(h)||_G
    <= eta[X_T(u_g)+X_T(u_h)]X_T(u_g-u_h)
    <= 4eta H||g-h||_G.                                (5.4)
If eta H<=1/8, Psi maps this ball into the smaller radius-H/2 ball and
has Lipschitz constant at most 1/2. The ball is nonempty and complete.
Banach's fixed-point theorem supplies an actual g*=Psi(g*).

The condition eta H<=1/8 is exactly
  T <= nu^3/(4096 C0^4 H^4)
     = nu^3/(4096 S0^6 H^4)
     = (27/2^24)nu^3/H^4.                              (5.5)
For H>0 choose T=T0 from (0.1). Since sqrt(nu)g*=-B(u,u), the field
u=u_g* satisfies
  u(t)=exp(nu t Delta)a
       -integral_0^t exp(nu(t-s)Delta)P((u.grad)u)(s) ds,
and
  u_t-nu Delta u+P((u.grad)u)=0                        (5.6)
in L2_tL2_x. Equations (3.2)--(3.7) supply all regularity and the strong
initial trace in the target. In particular X_T(u)<=2H.
One sufficient preliminary low-frequency bound is
  sup_t||u(t)||_2<=M+sqrt(nu T)H.
The energy identity in section 7 improves this to M.

6. PRESSURE AND THE PHYSICAL UNPROJECTED EQUATION

Since H1 embeds into L4 by L2/L6 interpolation, u in C_tH1 implies
u tensor u in C_tL2(R3;R^(3x3)). Define the scalar pressure by the bounded
Fourier multiplier
  Fp(t,xi)=-sum_(i,j) [xi_i xi_j/|xi|^2] F(u_i u_j)(t,xi),
with symbol zero at xi=0. The contraction of a tensor against the rank-one
unit tensor has operator norm<=1, so p in C_tL2 and is real. This is the
usual p=sum R_i R_j(u_i u_j) with the corresponding Riesz convention.

Distributional solenoidality and the H1 product rule identify
div(u tensor u)=(u.grad)u. Consequently
  grad p=-(I-P)((u.grad)u),
which belongs to L2_tL2_x by (4.1). Equation (5.6) becomes the actual
unprojected NSE with the same velocity and viscosity. Pressure is fixed
in the spatial L2 gauge; adding a function of time leaves the gradient.

7. PHYSICAL ENERGY AND ENSTROPHY ON THE CONSTRUCTED SLAB

The regularity already proved gives u in H1(0,T;L2) intersect L2(0,T;H2).
Hilbert-valued FTC and smooth approximation give absolute continuity of
  E(t)=||u(t)||_2^2,  Y(t)=||grad u(t)||_2^2,
and, for almost every t,
  E'=2<u,u_t>,  Y'=-2<Delta u,u_t>.                    (7.1)
The second identity follows first for Fourier/time smooth approximants
and then by convergence of u_t and Delta u in L2; the product pairings
converge in L1. The C_tH1 trace from section 3 identifies the endpoint values.

At a.e. time the convection cancellation is justified on R3 by compact
cutoffs: u in H1 implies u in L3 and L4; the product chain rule holds in
L1, and the boundary error is at most (C/R) integral_|x|>R |u|^3 ->0.
Thus <u,(u.grad)u>=0. The pressure/gradient part vanishes against the
solenoidal velocity. Spatial weak integration by parts yields
  E'(t)=-2nu Y(t),
  E(t)+2nu integral_0^t Y(s)ds=E(0).                   (7.2)
This proves sup_t||u(t)||_2<=M and supplies a genuine physical energy
equality for this constructed local solution.

Set Z=||Delta u||_2^2 and N=|<(u.grad)u,Delta u>|. The same estimates give
  N<=S0^(3/2)Y^(3/4)Z^(3/4),
  N^4<=S0^6 Y^3 Z^3,
  Y'+2nu Z<=2N.
The separate exact scalar polynomial derivation then gives
  Y'+nu Z <= (27S0^6/(16nu^3))Y^3
           =256nu^(-3)Y^3.                             (7.3)
This is the physical nonsharp coefficient for this construction. The
registered pi^(-4) coefficient additionally needs sharp S6 admission.
No identification of S0 with S6 is made.

8. UNIQUENESS IN THE ENTIRE STRONG CLASS

Take any two solutions u,v in C([0,T];H1_sigma) intersect L2(0,T;H2)
with u_t,v_t in L2_tL2 and the same initial datum. Their projected PDEs
imply the Duhamel formula by the linear heat equation. If pressure was
initially supplied in the unprojected equation, the other terms make its
gradient L2; the L2 Helmholtz projection therefore removes it.

On an interval I=[s,s+h] on which u(s)=v(s), put w=u-v. Then
  w=-K_nu[B(w,u)+B(v,w)]
with zero initial trace on that interval. Equations (3.6),(4.1) imply
  X_I(w)<=C0 nu^(-3/4)h^(1/4)
               [X_I(u)+X_I(v)] X_I(w).                (8.1)
The quantities X_I(u),X_I(v) are bounded by finite global-slab quantities.
Choose one h>0 making the coefficient in (8.1) strictly below 1. It follows
that X_I(w)=0. Since each w(t) is L2 and its gradient is zero, w(t)=0.
Partition [0,T] into finitely many intervals of this length and repeat.
This proves velocity uniqueness without assuming an arbitrary competitor
is in the original fixed-point ball. It also proves overlap uniqueness.

9. INTERIOR RESTART, MAXIMAL DEVELOPMENT AND CONTINUATION

At every t0 within an existing lifespan, the actual trace u(t0) belongs to
H1_sigma by C_tH1. Translate time and repeat the construction from that
datum with the same nu. Uniqueness glues the new solution to the old one
on the overlap. Piecewise time derivatives do not create a delta at the
join because the H1 (hence L2) traces agree.

Take the union of all such uniquely compatible local developments and its
supremal lifetime Tmax in (0,infinity]. Every compact subinterval inherits
the physical strong regularity, equation, initial trace and energy identity.

If Tmax<infinity and ||grad u(t)||_2<=K for all t<Tmax, then K=0 gives the
zero continuation, while K>0 gives a uniform restart interval
  delta=c0 nu^3/K^4>0.
Choose t0<Tmax so close that Tmax-t0<delta. The local constructor at t0
extends past Tmax and agrees with the existing trajectory. This contradicts
maximality. No trace at Tmax is assumed and no limit of pointwise H1 bounds
is used to create such a trace. Bounded full H1 norm is therefore sufficient;
indeed (7.2) already controls the missing L2 term along the same trajectory.

10. ALTERNATIVE FINITE-ENDPOINT SQUARE-BUDGET EXCLUSION

There is a useful consequence independent of the sharp Sobolev coefficient.
If Tmax<infinity then u(t) cannot have zero gradient at any t<Tmax, since
that L2 datum would be zero and admit a global zero continuation. The local
lifespan bound at each interior trace and maximality give
  c0 nu^3/||grad u(t)||_2^4 <= Tmax-t,
so
  ||grad u(t)||_2^4 >= c0 nu^3/(Tmax-t),  0<=t<Tmax.     (10.1)
For 0<=R<Tmax integration yields
  integral_0^R ||grad u(t)||_2^4 dt
    >=c0 nu^3 log[Tmax/(Tmax-R)].                       (10.2)
The right side tends to infinity as R increases to Tmax. Thus the full
nonnegative terminal budget is infinite:
  integral^+_(0,Tmax) ||grad u(t)||_2^4 dt = infinity.   (10.3)

For the registered stopped horizon b_A=min(Tmax,tau_A), a finite budget
therefore excludes Tmax<=tau_A whenever the cutoff is positive. This is an
alternative P15 implication based on quantitative G01 local theory. It
does not alone prove globality after tau_A: the registered crossing and
small-product continuation argument still needs its actual physical inputs.
The zero datum is handled separately as in section 0.
