1. Exact input on a finite closed interval [0,R].
Let Y: R -> R, R>=0, and kappa>=0. Assume Y absolutely continuous on
[0,R], and Y'(r)<=kappa Y(r)^3 for almost every r in (0,R). Endpoints
need no classical derivative. Absolute continuity supplies continuity and
an integrable a.e. derivative on every closed subinterval [0,t].

2. Restriction to each closed subinterval.
For t in [0,R], absolute continuity restricts to [0,t]. Restrict the a.e.
cubic inequality to (0,t); singleton-nullity transfers it to [0,t].
Both Y' and kappa Y^3 are integrable on [0,t]. The first follows from AC;
the second follows from continuity. Thus integral monotonicity is valid.

3. Actual AC-to-Volterra edge.
The AC fundamental theorem gives
  Y(t)-Y(0) = integral_0^t Y'(r) dr
           <= integral_0^t kappa Y(r)^3 dr.
Consequently Y(t)<=Y(0)+integral_0^t kappa Y(r)^3 dr. This integral
inequality is derived, not placed in the input contract.

4. Existing named-audited Gronwall application.
Take a(r)=kappa Y(r)^2, e(r)=Y(r), delta=Y(0). The coefficient is continuous
and nonnegative. The product a(r)e(r)=kappa Y(r)^3 is integrable. ES03,
QFA.Endpoints.Comparison.CRITICAL_RELATIVE_ENERGY_GRONWALL, therefore gives
  Y(t)<=Y(0) exp(kappa integral_0^t Y(r)^2 dr).
ES02 and ES03 are the exact source anchors. No continuity of a separate
classical derivative or Laplacian function is required.

5. Closed-window uniform bound.
If Y(0)>=0 and integral_0^R Y^2<=M, nonnegative integral monotonicity gives
integral_0^t Y^2<=M. Monotonicity of exp and multiplication by Y(0) yield
  Y(t)<=Y(0) exp(kappa M), 0<=t<=R.
There is no assumption of monotonicity of Y itself.

6. Full stopped budget, preserving infinity.
For real b define
  J(Y,b) := lintegral_{(0,b)} ENNReal.ofReal(Y(r)^2) dr : ENNReal.
At b=0 and for the zero function this is zero. Suppose J(Y,b) is finite.
For 0<=t<b, if Y is continuous on [0,t], the ordinary nonnegative integral
on [0,t] has ofReal equal to its nonnegative integral on (0,t]. Since
(0,t] is contained in (0,b), monotonicity gives
  ofReal(integral_0^t Y^2)<=J(Y,b).
Only after using the explicit J(Y,b)!=infinity premise do we apply toReal:
  integral_0^t Y^2 <= J(Y,b).toReal.
No finiteness is inferred from compact-subinterval finiteness alone.

7. Bound uniform up to the full open endpoint.
Assume for every R with 0<=R<b that Y is absolutely continuous on [0,R],
and assume the a.e. cubic inequality on (0,b), kappa>=0, Y(0)>=0, and
J(Y,b)!=infinity. For each t in [0,b), apply step 4 to [0,t] and step 6.
The SAME finite number
  B = Y(0) exp(kappa J(Y,b).toReal)
satisfies B>=0 and Y(t)<=B for every t in [0,b).
This endpoint is valid even if Y has no value or regularity at b.

8. First remaining physical obstruction.
For NSE, the intended substitution is Y(t)=||grad U(t)||_2^2 and
kappa=pi^(-4)*nu^(-3), nu>0. Constructing U and proving AC and the a.e.
cubic inequality with the registered spatial constant remain G01/G02/G03
obligations. A scalar bound does not prove an H1 continuation criterion,
full QFA/WFA, the literal Fefferman-A bridge, or universal budget finiteness.
No derivation beyond that obstruction is claimed.
