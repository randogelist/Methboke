0. EXACT STATEMENT TO IDENTIFY

Fix nu>0 and real solenoidal a in Schwartz(R3;R3). Let U_a be its canonical
maximal H1 strong development with lifetime Tmax(a,nu), constructed by the
G01 local/maximal theory. The intended P19 equivalence is

  Tmax(a,nu)=infinity
  <=> there exists a literal global smooth finite-energy solution (u,p)
      of unforced viscosity-nu NSE with u(0)=a.             (0.1)

Here literal finite energy means the velocity has a uniform global L2
energy bound as required in the registered Fefferman-A category. On every
finite closed time slab the competitor is jointly smooth through its final
time. No full H1 or H2 bound is added to that literal category as a premise.
Pressure may initially have any time-dependent additive constant.

2. ARBITRARY VISCOSITY TRANSPORT

For a viscosity-nu solution on [0,T], set tau=nu t and
  v(tau,x)=nu^(-1)u(tau/nu,x),
  q(tau,x)=nu^(-2)p(tau/nu,x).                           (2.1)
Then v has viscosity one, initial datum a/nu, and closed interval [0,nu T].
Indeed each original term divided by nu^2 becomes the corresponding term
in v_tau-Delta v+(v.grad)v+grad q=0. Divergence remains zero.
Multiplication by fixed positive constants and the linear time change
preserve smoothness, finite-energy membership and every finite-slab Sobolev
class needed below. The datum a/nu is still Schwartz and solenoidal.
Thus T1--T5 can be applied at viscosity one and transported back to nu.

3. FORWARD DIRECTION: GLOBAL CANONICAL DEVELOPMENT TO LITERAL SOLUTION

Assume Tmax(a,nu)=infinity. Fix any finite T>0. The canonical solution
belongs to C([0,T];H1_sigma) intersect L2(0,T;H2) and satisfies its actual
Duhamel equation with normalized pressure. Apply (2.1), then T4 to the
Schwartz datum and zero forcing. Pulling back gives a jointly smooth
velocity and normalized pressure on the whole closed physical slab [0,T].

The pressure is given by the same Fourier multiplier of u tensor u on
overlapping intervals. Velocity uniqueness makes the local representations
agree. Since T was arbitrary, these define one smooth pair on [0,infinity).
The physical local energy identity derived in section 7 of
H1_LOCAL_FIXED_POINT_CHAIN.txt along this same canonical trajectory implies
  ||U_a(t)||_2^2 + 2nu integral_0^t ||grad U_a(s)||_2^2 ds
     =||a||_2^2
for each finite t. In particular the global uniform L2 bound is ||a||_2^2.
The resulting pair satisfies the actual PDE and datum required in (0.1).

This step uses all-order persistence through t=0 for Schwartz input. It
does not replace that input by arbitrary smooth finite-energy data, for
which the same strong time-smoothness assertion would need qualification.

4. REVERSE DIRECTION: LITERAL SOLUTION TO CANONICAL GLOBALITY

Assume a literal global smooth finite-energy pair (u,p) with datum a exists.
Suppose for contradiction that the canonical maximal lifetime Tmax is finite.
Choose a finite R>Tmax. The competitor exists and is jointly smooth on the
CLOSED slab [0,R], with a finite uniform L2 bound there. Its initial datum
is H1 and its forcing is zero. Apply viscosity transport (2.1) and T1.
Returning to physical time gives one finite number K_R such that
  ||u||_(L-infinity(0,R;H1)) + ||u||_(L2(0,R;H2)) <= K_R. (4.1)

This is the terminal-control step. The bound is for a fixed closed slab
containing Tmax in its interior, so it is uniform as the canonical time
approaches Tmax from below. It is not obtained by taking unrelated bounds
for every shorter canonical slab.

By T3, normalize pressure without altering u and obtain an H1 mild solution
on [0,R]. At this stage the pressure-gradient equality and additive gauge
are asserted only for almost every time; section 5 upgrades them after T4.
Let u_m be the strong H1-continuous representative supplied by T4. To
identify it with the literal field at every time, fix any smooth compactly
supported spatial vector test phi. The function
  t |-> integral_R3 u_m(t,x).phi(x) dx
is continuous by C_tH1, and
  t |-> integral_R3 u(t,x).phi(x) dx
is continuous by joint smoothness on the fixed compact support of phi.
They agree for almost every time, hence for every t in [0,R], including
the endpoints by one-sided continuity. Thus u_m(t)=u(t) as spatial
distributions for every time, identifying the literal field with its H1
class without presuming global L2 continuity from local smoothness.
T4 with Schwartz data also gives a jointly smooth representative, so the
two spatially smooth fields agree pointwise at every time.

The H1 norm of u_m is continuous. Its essential supremum bound from (4.1)
therefore gives ||u_m(t)||_H1<=K_R for every t in [0,R]; otherwise continuity
would produce a positive-measure interval violating that essential bound.
For every s<Tmax, strong/mild uniqueness identifies u_m with U_a on [0,s].
Consequently the one fixed closed-slab estimate supplies the actual bound
  sup_(0<=t<Tmax)||U_a(t)||_H1 <= K_R.
The actual bounded-H1 continuation theorem from G01 then extends U_a past
Tmax, contradicting maximality. Therefore Tmax=infinity.

No initial global H1 premise was imposed on the literal competitor. Its
H1 admission came from the PDE theorem T1 using closed-terminal smoothness.
Pressure normalization keeps the original velocity, datum and viscosity.

5. ZERO DATUM AND GAUGE HANDLING

T3 initially identifies the original pressure gradient with the normalized
one only for almost every time. Apply T4 to the normalized mild solution
with Schwartz datum and zero forcing; its normalized pressure p_norm has a
jointly smooth representative on each closed slab. Both original p and
p_norm now have smooth spatial gradients. Pairing their gradient difference
with any compactly supported smooth spatial test gives a continuous scalar
function of time which vanishes almost everywhere. It therefore vanishes
at every time. Hence the gradients agree as distributions, and then as
smooth functions, at every point of the closed slab.

Connectedness of R3 makes p(t,.)-p_norm(t,.) spatially constant for each t.
Set
  c(t)=p(t,0)-p_norm(t,0).
This is a smooth function of time and
  p(t,x)=p_norm(t,x)+c(t)
holds at every spacetime point, including the initial and terminal times.
The statement concerns existence and velocity; it does not claim uniqueness
of an unnormalized scalar pressure.

For a=0, the canonical zero velocity with normalized zero pressure is
global and literal. The reverse argument remains valid; H1 uniqueness
identifies any literal competitor's velocity with zero after T1--T4, and
the same pressure-gauge argument applies.
