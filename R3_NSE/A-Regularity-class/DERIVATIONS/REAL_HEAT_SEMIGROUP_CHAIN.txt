real vector L2 space SpatialL2 and on the accepted physical weak-gradient

strictly positive-time smoothing, not for this semigroup or its continuity.

The Fourier convention is exp(-2*pi*i*x.xi). Set
  lambda(xi)=4*pi^2*|xi|^2,

The scalar identities are

3. STRONG CONTINUITY ON L2, WITH A GENUINE INTEGRABLE MAJORANT

Fix u in SpatialL2, nu>=0 and a reference time s>=0. Put
  f=F(complexify u),
  D(t,xi)=(h_nu(t,xi)-h_nu(s,xi))^2 |f(xi)|^2.
The actual L2 energy theorem gives
  integral |f(xi)|^2 dxi=||u||_2^2 < infinity.              (3.1)
Since both symbol values lie in [0,1],
  0<=D(t,xi)<=|f(xi)|^2                                  (3.2)
for every t,xi. Each D(t,.) is measurable, and for every fixed xi its
dependence on t is continuous. Dominated convergence therefore proves
continuity of t |-> integral D(t,xi)dxi on NNReal. At t=s that integral is
zero. Actual Fourier isometry and (0.2) identify, for every t,
  ||H_nu(t)u-H_nu(s)u||_2^2=integral D(t,xi)dxi.            (3.3)
Taking the continuous nonnegative square root gives strong continuity at s.
This includes strong convergence to the original datum as t decreases to
zero. It does not use operator-norm continuity; no such claim is made.

The proof needs no uniform compact Fourier support, no L1 hypothesis on u,
no Schwartz datum, no uniform frequency bound and no integral of a bare
constant over R3. The integrable majorant is the input's L2 energy density.

4. ACTUAL WEAK-GRADIENT TRANSPORT

Let u in PhysicalH1, write v for its actual L2 velocity and G_j for its
three actual weak-gradient fields. The accepted forward/reverse native H1
Fourier bridge says, simultaneously for all j,
  F(complexify G_j)(xi)
    =(2*pi*i*xi_j) F(complexify v)(xi)                  a.e. (4.1)
The scalar symbol commutes with this coordinate derivative multiplier.
Multiplying (4.1) by h_nu(t,xi) and using (0.2) proves the same identity for
  v_t=H_nu(t)v,   (G_t)_j=H_nu(t)G_j.
The reverse bridge gives the actual compact-test weak-gradient equations
for (v_t,G_t), hence a constructed physical H1 element. Denote it H1_nu(t)u.
Its velocity and derivative maps are literally those displayed fields.

The physical norm is inhomogeneous:
  ||u||_H1^2=||v||_2^2+sum_j||G_j||_2^2.
Applying L2 contraction to all four fields gives
  ||H1_nu(t)u||_H1 <= ||u||_H1,                            (4.2)
and separately
  E(H1_nu(t)u)<=E(u),  Y(H1_nu(t)u)<=Y(u).                (4.3)
Thus the low-frequency velocity term is retained. No whole-space Poincare
inequality is used. Additivity, real homogeneity, zero time, zero viscosity
