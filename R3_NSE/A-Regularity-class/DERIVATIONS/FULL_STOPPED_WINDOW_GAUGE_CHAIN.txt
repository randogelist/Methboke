0. TYPES, PREMISES AND CLAIM BOUNDARY

Let T belong to ENNReal=[0,infinity], let tau be a real number with
0<=tau, and let nu be a real number with 0<nu. Thus tau and nu are finite.
T is allowed to be zero or infinity. Write [c]=ENNReal.ofReal(c) for a
real c; we supply nonnegativity whenever its real arithmetic is used.
All budgets, their suprema and the gauge below have type ENNReal.
The exponent 1/4 denotes the real rpow exponent; a fourth power is a
natural-number power. These conventions also apply at zero and infinity.

Use actual PhysicalH1 elements, their actual L2 velocities and supplied
weak gradients. A time path may be given only on its existing lifetime
D_T={t:Real | 0<=t and [t]<T}, or on its positive-time part. Writing
U:Real->PhysicalH1 means an arbitrary extension of that path to other
times; every integral is restricted to the observation set. Its
value is unchanged if the extension is changed outside that set, or on
a null subset of that set. The zero element gives an available extension.

No NSE equation, canonical solution, uniqueness, restart or terminal
continuity is assumed here. Solenoidality is not required by these
pathwise identities. It may be retained when the supplied path has it.
The basic ENNReal definitions, comparison and Fourier normalization do
not need temporal measurability. For exact splitting into the six time
integrals, assume ContinuousOn U on the open observation set, or instead
assume the explicitly needed restricted a.e. measurability of the charges.
For finite ordinary integrals from zero, a separate compact-interval
continuity premise is stated in Section 4. No budget finiteness is a
premise of the definitions.

1. FINITE CUTOFF BEFORE toReal

First define beta=min(T,[tau]) in ENNReal. Since

  beta <= [tau] < infinity,

beta is finite. Only now set b=beta.toReal. Then

  0<=b<=tau,                 [b]=beta.

Define the open observation set exactly by

  O(T,tau)={t:Real | 0<t and [t]<T and t<tau}.

For a positive t, [t]<[tau] iff t<tau. Consequently

  t in O(T,tau)
    iff 0<t and [t]<min(T,[tau])
    iff 0<t and t<beta.toReal
    iff t in Ioo(0,b).                                      (W1)

The middle equivalence uses beta!=infinity and t>=0. Thus (W1) is
literal equality of sets, not merely equality modulo endpoint null sets.

Special cases are part of this construction:

  T=infinity       ==> beta=[tau], b=tau;
  T=0 or tau=0     ==> beta=0, b=0, O=empty;
  b=0             iff T=0 or tau=0;
  b>0             iff T>0 and tau>0.

If T is finite, b=min(T.toReal,tau) follows afterwards. That formula is
not valid without finiteness of T: ENNReal.toReal(infinity)=0, whereas
the correctly stopped endpoint when T=infinity is b=tau.

2. ACTUAL PHYSICAL CHARGES AND COMPLETE NONNEGATIVE BUDGETS

For t in O set

  Y(t)=physicalH1Enstrophy(U(t))
      =sum_(j:Fin 3) ||physicalH1Gradient j (U(t))||_2^2,
  q_i(t)=physicalH1SectorCharge i (U(t)),       i:Fin 6.

The q_i use the constructed physical H1 sector operators, not an abstract
family constrained by a supplied charge-sum hypothesis. Their native
pointwise results give

  0<=q_i(t),       sum_i q_i(t)=Y(t),       0<=Y(t),
  sum_i q_i(t)^2 <= Y(t)^2 <= 6*sum_i q_i(t)^2.              (P1)

Each q_i is already a squared gradient norm. Its square q_i^2 is a fourth
power of a gradient L2 norm. Use that square in the time budget.

Define, with Lebesgue volume on time,

  J   = integral^-_(t in O) [Y(t)^2],
  J6  = integral^-_(t in O) [sum_i q_i(t)^2],
  Ji  = integral^-_(t in O) [q_i(t)^2].

These are defined even if their value is infinity. By (W1), J is exactly
ACBudget.stoppedSquareBudget(Y,b), and J6 is exactly SixChargeBudget's
sixChargeBudget with the measure volume.restrict(Ioo(0,b)).

Monotonicity of the nonnegative integral, (P1), and extraction of the
finite coefficient 6 give

  J6 <= J <= 6*J6.                                          (P2)

This also holds for infinite values. Since multiplication by the finite
positive number 6 preserves finiteness,

  J<infinity iff J6<infinity.                               (P3)

If U is continuous on O, continuity of each actual charge supplies its
restricted a.e. measurability. The exact finite-sum integral theorem
then yields, with no finiteness premise,

  J6=sum_(i:Fin 6) Ji,
  J6<infinity iff every Ji<infinity.                        (P4)

The same statements hold under restricted a.e. measurability of the
charge functions. No additivity of lower integrals of arbitrary
nonmeasurable functions is silently used in (P4).

3. THE COMPLETE IMPROPER ENDPOINT IS A SUPREMUM

For a real r define partial budgets on Ioo(0,r):

  J(r)=integral^-_(0<t<r) [Y(t)^2],
  J6(r)=integral^-_(0<t<r) [sum_i q_i(t)^2],
  Ji(r)=integral^-_(0<t<r) [q_i(t)^2].

The budgets increase with r, by inclusion of intervals. For b>0 put

  r_n=b*(n+1)/(n+2),            n:Nat.

Then 0<r_n<b, r_n increases to b, and

  union_n Ioo(0,r_n)=Ioo(0,b).

For the nontrivial inclusion, given 0<t<b choose n large enough that
b/(n+2)<b-t. This gives t<r_n. Countable directed-union continuity of
the nonnegative integral therefore gives

  J=sup_n J(r_n)=sup_{0<=r<b} J(r).                         (E1)

To justify the second equality, each admissible r has J(r)<=J, whereas
every r_n is admissible. The same proof applies to J6 and each Ji.
When b=0, all r_n=0, their intervals are empty, and both sides of (E1)
are zero; the supremum over {r | 0<=r<b} is the empty supremum, also zero.

The pinned setLIntegral_iUnion_of_directed theorem requires countability
and directedness of the sets; it requires no measurability premise on
the integrand or sets. Thus (E1) is available already at the general
definition level. Under temporal measurability the usual monotone
convergence theorem is an alternative proof. The increasing sequence
of partial budgets converges to the displayed supremum in ENNReal;
for b>0 this is the full improper limit approaching b from below.
This limit and its supremum may be infinity.

There is an exact uniform-bound characterization:

  J<infinity
    iff exists C:NNReal, for every 0<=r<b, J(r)<=C
    iff exists C:NNReal, for every n:Nat, J(r_n)<=C.         (E2)

Here NNReal C is included in ENNReal, so it is necessarily finite. The
forward direction takes C=J.toNNReal only after J!=infinity has been
established. The reverse direction takes the supremum of the common
bound and uses (E1). Equivalently one can take a single natural-number
bound, since any finite ENNReal value is smaller than some natural.

E2 must not be replaced by the weaker assertion

  for every 0<=r<b, J(r)<infinity.                         (NOT E2)

For an actual H1 counterexample, let T=tau=b=1 and choose a fixed smooth
compactly supported real vector field w with Y(w)>0, viewed as its
actual PhysicalH1 element. On 0<=t<1 put

  U(t)=(1-t)^(-1/4) * w.

This path is continuous on every [0,r] with r<1. Quadratic homogeneity
of enstrophy gives

  Y(t)^2=Y(w)^2/(1-t),
  J(r)=[Y(w)^2*(-log(1-r))] < infinity     for 0<=r<1,
  J=infinity.

The common bound in (E2) fails. This example is a supplied H1 path and
claims no NSE solution property. It also shows why no terminal value
or terminal continuity may be inserted to make finiteness automatic.

4. ORDINARY PARTIAL INTEGRALS AND THE INITIAL ENDPOINT

If U is continuous on every [0,r] with 0<=r<b, then Y^2 and sum_i q_i^2
are continuous there. A compact interval has finite volume, so their
ordinary integrals are finite and agree with the ENNReal partial budgets:

  J(r)=[integral_(0<t<r) Y(t)^2 dt],
  J6(r)=[integral_(0<t<r) sum_i q_i(t)^2 dt].                (E3)

Replacing Ioo(0,r) by the closed interval [0,r] changes no integral.
The ordinary integral over 0..r agrees as well because r>=0. These
identifications use integrability and nonnegativity, not the default
value of a totalized Bochner integral on a nonintegrable function.
Together (E1) and (E3) recover the registered full improper integral
without requiring a value, continuity or an H1 bound at b.

Continuity merely on (0,b) does not imply integrability near zero.
Under that weaker premise use, for b>0, the nested compact exhaustion

  K_n=[b/(n+2), b*(n+1)/(n+2)] subset (0,b).

Its union is (0,b). Each compact integral is finite and their supremum
is the same full budget. For b=0 use the already proved empty-window
case. This avoids silently assuming a continuous extension at the
initial time when the supplied path did not include one.

5. THE ACTUAL H1 FOURIER FACTOR

For each t, take the actual L2 Fourier transform of the complexification
of the physical velocity of U(t), with phase exp(-2*pi*i*x dot xi). Let

  Q(t)=integral_(xi in R3) |xi|^2 * |Fourier(U(t))(xi)|^2 dxi,
  F(t)=integral^-_(xi in R3) [|xi|^2 * |Fourier(U(t))(xi)|^2].

H1Spectral proves integrability of this actual spatial weighted density
from the supplied weak gradient. Therefore Q(t) is a genuine finite
nonnegative real integral and

  F(t)=[Q(t)]<infinity,       Y(t)=(4*pi^2)*Q(t).            (F1)

No temporal integrability, chosen jointly measurable Fourier
representatives, Laplacian premise or spectral-admission assumption is
needed here. All identities are independent of a.e. representative
choices in space. When U is continuous on O, Q=Y/(4*pi^2) is continuous
there; consequently the spectral time density is measurable without a
separate joint-representative construction.

With c=[4*pi^2], (F1) yields

  [Y(t)]=c*F(t),
  [Y(t)^2]=[16*pi^4]*F(t)^2.                              (F2)

Define the Fourier time budget by the full nonnegative integral

  B=integral^-_(t in O) F(t)^2
   =integral^-_(t in O) [Q(t)^2].

The coefficient [16*pi^4] is finite, so it can be extracted from the
integral even without temporal measurability. Hence

  J=[16*pi^4]*B,               B=J/[16*pi^4].              (F3)

The coefficient is strictly positive. Thus (F3) holds both for finite
values and for J=B=infinity, and

  B<infinity iff J<infinity.                               (F4)

The same normalization holds on every partial interval and every compact
interior interval. In particular, B is the supremum of its complete
family of nonnegative partial budgets, exactly as in (E1).

The six charges have the compatible literal spatial description

  q_i(t)=(4*pi^2)*integral_(xi in sixSector i)
                         |xi|^2 * |Fourier(U(t))(xi)|^2 dxi.

This uses the same actual unprojected velocity and exact six sectors as
PhysicalH1Sectors.physicalH1SectorCharge_eq_sector_integral. It does not
substitute the sum of unsquared charges for the six-charge budget.

6. GAUGE, FOURTH POWER AND FINITENESS INCLUDING INFINITY

Put k=[4*nu]. Since nu>0, 0<k<infinity. Define directly in ENNReal

  L=(k*J)^(1/4).

There is no toReal operation in this definition. The ENNReal fourth-root
identity, valid at zero and infinity as well as at finite positive values,
gives

  L^4=k*J=[64*pi^4*nu]*B.                                  (G1)

In particular,

  J=0        ==> L=0,
  J=infinity ==> k*J=infinity ==> L=infinity,
  L<infinity iff J<infinity iff J6<infinity iff B<infinity.  (G2)

Under the restricted measurability premise of (P4), these are also
equivalent to every individual Ji being finite. Thus there are no
unrecorded finite-value restrictions in the normalization or the
finiteness equivalences.

Multiplying (P2) by k gives the registered gauge comparison

  [4*nu]*J6 <= L^4 <= [24*nu]*J6.                          (G3)

If L6=(k*J6)^(1/4), monotonicity and the nonnegative product-rpow rule give

  L6<=L<=6^(1/4)*L6.                                      (G4)

The positivity of nu is essential to (G2). At nu=0, the ENNReal convention
0*infinity=0 would make this gauge zero even for an infinite J. The
physical budget identities and (P2),(F3) themselves do not require nu.

7. ZERO WINDOW AND ZERO PATH

If T=0 or tau=0, then O is empty and

  J=J6=Ji=B=L=L6=0.

These formulas hold for every supplied path, whatever its values outside
O. If U=0 on O, or U=0 almost everywhere for the restricted time measure,
the actual linear gradient and sector maps vanish there, so the same
zero formulas hold on a nonempty window. The Fourier formula agrees by
(F1). No evolution-uniqueness assertion is needed to prove these cases.

For a later cutoff tau(a,nu)=8*||a||_2^4/(27*pi^4*nu^5), zero data give
tau=0 and hence the zero-window formula. This is a scalar substitution;
constructing the canonical zero NSE trajectory is a separate endpoint.

8. SUBLEVELS AND INTEGER EXHAUSTION

Let D be any family of valid supplied-path inputs d=(T_d,tau_d,U_d),
with fixed nu>0. Define, for a finite real radius R>=0,

  C_R={d in D | L(d)<=[R]},
  C_fin={d in D | L(d)<infinity}.

If 0<=R<=S, then C_R subset C_S. Positive finite coefficient cancellation
and the fourth-root order equivalence give the exact sublevel formulas

  L(d)<=[R]
    iff J(d)<=[R^4/(4*nu)]
    iff B(d)<=[R^4/(64*pi^4*nu)].                          (S1)

This includes R=0. Infinite budgets are automatically excluded from
every such finite-radius sublevel; no preliminary toReal of L, J or B
is used in (S1). From (P2), for every finite real C>=0,

  {d | J6(d)<=[C/6]} subset {d | J(d)<=[C]}
                         subset {d | J6(d)<=[C]}.         (S2)

Every finite ENNReal value is bounded by a natural number. Therefore

  C_fin=union_(n:Nat) C_n=union_(n:Nat) C_(n+1).           (S3)

For the forward inclusion choose n>L(d) using finiteness; for the reverse
inclusion use L(d)<=n<infinity. By (G2), the same C_fin is the finite-J,
finite-J6 and finite-B class, and under (P4) the all-Ji-finite class.
These are set-theoretic sublevels and an exhaustion, with no assertion
of compactness, a norm structure or an initial-data-only membership test.

For the same compatible path and fixed nu, enlarging T or tau enlarges
O; consequently J,J6,B and L are monotone under such window inclusion.
This does not assert monotonicity with respect to nu when the path,
lifetime or cutoff changes with viscosity.
