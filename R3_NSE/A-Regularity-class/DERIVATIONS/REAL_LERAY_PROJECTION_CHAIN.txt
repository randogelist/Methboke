1. THE MEASURABLE MATRIX SYMBOL, INCLUDING ZERO FREQUENCY

For xi in R3 and z in C3 put d_xi(z)=sum_j xi_j*z_j. For xi!=0 define
  R_xi z = xi * d_xi(z)/|xi|^2,       P_xi z = z-R_xi z;
at xi=0 set R_0=0 and P_0=I. Real vectors are embedded coordinatewise.
Each P_xi is complex-linear. Away from zero, d_xi(xi)=|xi|^2, so
  R_xi^2=R_xi, P_xi^2=P_xi, P_xi R_xi=R_xi P_xi=0,
  d_xi(P_xi z)=0,                    P_xi z=z iff d_xi(z)=0.
The last equivalence also holds at zero, where both conditions are automatic.
The matrices are real symmetric, hence Hermitian. Orthogonality gives
  |P_xi z|^2+|R_xi z|^2=|z|^2,
  |R_xi z|^2=|d_xi(z)|^2/|xi|^2       when xi!=0.
Thus both symbols have operator norm at most one, including at zero.
Their entries are Borel: rational continuous expressions away from the
closed singleton {0}, with the explicitly assigned values on that set.
Also P_(-xi)=P_xi and conjugate(P_xi z)=P_xi(conjugate z).
No zero-mean condition is imposed; a singleton frequency value changes
no L2 class. No singular distribution is multiplied by this symbol.
