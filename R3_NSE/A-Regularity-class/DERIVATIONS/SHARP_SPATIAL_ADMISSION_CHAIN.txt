0. EXACT SPATIAL TARGET AND NORMALIZATION

All spatial integrals below use full ordinary Lebesgue measure on R3.
Every finite-dimensional fiber is Euclidean, and all tensor norms are
Frobenius norms. Spatial weak derivatives are actual distributional weak
derivatives. Let S6>0 be the positive constant satisfying
  S6^6=16/(27 pi^4).                                      (0.1)

The target admissions are:

  ||F||_6 <= S6 ||grad F||_2                              (0.2)
for every physical H1 field with any finite-dimensional Euclidean fiber;

  ||grad u||_6 <= S6 ||Delta u||_2                        (0.3)
for every physical H2 velocity u:R3->R3; and consequently

  |integral_R3 ((u.grad)u).Delta u|^4
    <= S6^6 ||grad u||_2^6 ||Delta u||_2^6.               (0.4)

No solenoidality is needed for these three purely spatial inequalities.
Actual NSE evolution, pressure cancellation and the scalar time derivative
belong to other admissions. The frozen S6 and cutoff tau_A are not changed.

1. PRECISE ESTABLISHED SCALAR INPUT

[T] G. Talenti, Best constant in Sobolev inequality,
Annali di Matematica Pura ed Applicata 110 (1976), 353--372.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02418013
Original-paper PDF: https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/talenti.sobolev.pdf
Location: opening theorem and equations (1)--(3), printed pp.353--354.
The original displayed formula was checked directly in the page image.

Use only this scalar consequence of the theorem:
for every real f in C_c^infinity(R3),
  (integral |f|^6)^(1/6)
    <= C(3,2) (integral |grad f|^2)^(1/2),               (1.1)
where the theorem's full-space constant is
  C(m,p)=pi^(-1/2) m^(-1/p)
    ((p-1)/(m-p))^(1-1/p)
    [Gamma(1+m/2) Gamma(m) /
      (Gamma(m/p) Gamma(1+m-m/p))]^(1/m).                (1.2)
The gradient length and measure in [T] are the usual Euclidean ones.
This is an established analytical theorem, not a located native Lean proof.

At m=3,p=2, the factor involving (p-1)/(m-p) is one, and Gamma(5/2)
cancels between numerator and denominator. Since Gamma(3)=2 and
Gamma(3/2)=sqrt(pi)/2,
  C(3,2)=pi^(-1/2) 3^(-1/2) (4/sqrt(pi))^(1/3).
Every factor is positive, and taking the sixth power gives
  C(3,2)^6=pi^(-3) 3^(-3) (4/sqrt(pi))^2
          =16/(27 pi^4).                                (1.3)
Thus C(3,2)=S6, by uniqueness of the positive sixth root. Equivalently,
the coefficient in the squared lower-bound convention is
  ||grad f||_2^2 >= 3(pi/2)^(4/3) ||f||_6^2.
These reciprocal conventions must not be confused when admitting S6.

2. INDEPENDENT NORMALIZATION CHECK, INCLUDING THE L2 LIMITATION

For U(x)=(1+|x|^2)^(-1/2), radial integration and r=tan(theta) give
  integral_R3 |U|^6
    =4pi integral_0^infinity r^2/(1+r^2)^3 dr=pi^2/4,
  integral_R3 |grad U|^2
    =4pi integral_0^infinity r^4/(1+r^2)^3 dr=3pi^2/4.
Consequently the sixth power of its Sobolev quotient is
  (pi^2/4)/(3pi^2/4)^3=16/(27 pi^4).                    (2.1)

U is not L2 on R3, so it is not an extremizer in the inhomogeneous H1
carrier. To compare with compact tests, let chi_R be a smooth cutoff equal
to one on B_R, supported in B_(2R), and with |grad chi_R|<=C/R.
Then chi_R U is smooth and compactly supported, converges to U in L6,
and its gradient converges to grad U in L2. For the extra cutoff term,
  ||U grad chi_R||_2^2
    <= (C^2/R^2) integral_(R<|x|<2R) (1+|x|^2)^(-1) dx
    =O(R^(-1)) -> 0.
Thus the compact-test quotients approach (2.1). This checks the exact
normalization and sharpness in that class. A single extremizer computation
alone would not establish the upper inequality (1.1); [T] supplies it.

3. SMOOTH KATO REGULARIZATION: NO VECTOR OR TENSOR LOSS

Let F:R3->E be smooth and compactly supported, where E is any finite-
dimensional real Euclidean space. For epsilon>0 define the scalar function
  g_epsilon(x)=sqrt(|F(x)|^2+epsilon^2)-epsilon.           (3.1)
The positive epsilon removes the norm singularity at F=0. The function is
smooth everywhere and vanishes wherever F vanishes, so it has compact
support. It satisfies 0<=g_epsilon<=|F| and converges pointwise to |F|.

For each coordinate i, the ordinary chain rule gives
  partial_i g_epsilon
    =inner(F,partial_i F)/sqrt(|F|^2+epsilon^2).
Cauchy-Schwarz followed by summation over the three spatial directions
gives the Kato estimate in the exact Frobenius convention:
  |grad g_epsilon|^2
    <= [|F|^2/(|F|^2+epsilon^2)] sum_i |partial_i F|^2
    <= |grad F|^2.                                     (3.2)
There is no factor depending on dim(E), because the fiber inner product
is used before summing spatial directions.

Apply the scalar theorem (1.1) to g_epsilon, raise to the sixth power,
and use (3.2):
  integral |g_epsilon|^6
    <= S6^6 (integral |grad F|^2)^3.
Fatou along any positive epsilon_n->0 yields
  integral |F|^6 <= S6^6 (integral |grad F|^2)^3.         (3.3)
Taking nonnegative sixth roots gives (0.2) for compact smooth E-valued F.
This applies equally to velocity fibers R3 and gradient tensor fibers R9.
It does not pass through a componentwise triangle bound with a lost factor.

4. DENSITY INTO THE ACTUAL PHYSICAL H1 GRAPH

Let F and its three supplied actual weak derivatives be L2. Use the same
cutoffs chi_R as above. Weak product differentiation gives
  partial_i(chi_R F)=chi_R partial_i F+(partial_i chi_R)F.
The L2 tails of F and partial_i F tend to zero, and
  ||(grad chi_R)F||_2 <= (C/R)||F||_2 -> 0.
Therefore chi_R F->F in the full physical H1 graph norm. Mollify each
cutoff field by one common scalar approximate identity. Convolution
commutes with its distributional derivatives and approximates each L2
component. A diagonal choice gives F_n in C_c^infinity(R3;E) with
  F_n->F in L2 and grad F_n->grad F in L2.               (4.1)

A subsequence converges pointwise almost everywhere to F. Apply (3.3)
to that subsequence and Fatou; the gradient norm converges by (4.1).
The result is the same inequality (3.3) for this actual weak field, with
the same S6. Thus F belongs to L6 and (0.2) is established on physical H1.
Applying the inequality to differences also shows that H1 graph convergence
implies L6 convergence. No prior L6 membership was used in this admission.

The compact approximation need not preserve solenoidality: (0.2) is valid
for every H1 field, so no solenoidal density theorem is required here.
It does require density for the actual weak derivative graph, not merely
density in velocity L2 or a graph supplied with unrelated derivative fields.

5. PHYSICAL HESSIAN-LAPLACIAN IDENTITY AND ADMISSION

5.1 Identity on actual H2 fields.
Use the native Fourier convention exp(-2pi i x.xi), and put k=2pi xi.
For every component u_a of an actual H2 field,
  Fourier(partial_i partial_j u_a)=-k_i k_j Fourier(u_a),
  Fourier(Delta u_a)=-|k|^2 Fourier(u_a).
Plancherel and finite summation therefore give
  ||D2u||_2^2
    =integral sum_(a,i,j) k_i^2 k_j^2 |Fourier(u_a)|^2
    =integral |k|^4 |Fourier(u)|^2
    =||Delta u||_2^2.                                  (5.1)
All ordered pairs (i,j) are included. A convention counting each mixed
derivative just once without its multiplicity would not give (5.1).
The 2pi factors occur on both sides and cancel; no rescaling of S6 occurs.
No divergence-free premise, boundary condition or Poincare inequality is
used on this full-space identity.

The gradient tensor G=grad u is physical H1, with
  ||grad G||_2=||D2u||_2=||Delta u||_2.
Applying the R9-valued version of (0.2) to G proves (0.3).

5.2 If a caller supplies only an L2 Laplacian.
For u in L2 and actual distributional Delta u=d in L2, the Fourier identity
is Fourier(d)=-|k|^2 Fourier(u). It supplies
  integral |k|^4 |Fourier(u)|^2=||d||_2^2<infinity.
Since |k|^2<=1+|k|^4, the first derivative multipliers are L2 as well;
the velocity L2 term controls the low frequencies. For second derivatives,
each product k_i k_j Fourier(u) is L2 because |k_i k_j|<=|k|^2.
Inverse Fourier transformation constructs actual L2 derivative witnesses,
and the distributional Fourier derivative identity identifies them as
partial_i u and partial_i partial_j u. Hence u belongs to physical H2,
Delta u=d and (5.1) holds.

An equivalent bounded-symbol second-derivative construction multiplies
Fourier(d) by k_i k_j/|k|^2 away from zero, and defines that symbol as zero
at the origin. This symbol has absolute value at most one and is real/even.
It therefore preserves the Hermitian symmetry of real fields. Its zero
frequency value does not affect the L2 class. The first derivatives still
need the preceding low-frequency argument; division by |k| at zero is not
a substitute for it.

6. THE EXACT FOURTH-POWER INPUT TO ENSTROPHYCOEFFICIENT

For physical H2 u, Holder interpolation gives
  ||grad u||_3
    <= ||grad u||_2^(1/2)||grad u||_6^(1/2)
    <= S6^(1/2)||grad u||_2^(1/2)||Delta u||_2^(1/2).
The pointwise tensor contraction satisfies |(u.grad)u|<=|u||grad u|.
Combining the L6-L3-L2 Holder inequality with (0.2)--(0.3) gives
  N:=|integral ((u.grad)u).Delta u|
    <= ||u||_6||grad u||_3||Delta u||_2
    <= S6^(3/2)||grad u||_2^(3/2)||Delta u||_2^(3/2).
All these products are integrable: (u.grad)u is L2 by the displayed bounds,
and Delta u is L2. With Y=||grad u||_2^2 and Z=||Delta u||_2^2, raising
the nonnegative inequality to the fourth power gives exactly
  N^4<=S6^6 Y^3 Z^3.                                   (6.1)

This establishes the spatial input, relative to the scalar theorem [T].
The separate actual time-enstrophy inequality d+2nu Z<=2N is still needed
to invoke the scalar native module. Once it is supplied, that module's
coefficient becomes 27S6^6/(16nu^3)=1/(pi^4 nu^3).
The spatial derivation alone supplies neither d=deriv Y nor time AC.
