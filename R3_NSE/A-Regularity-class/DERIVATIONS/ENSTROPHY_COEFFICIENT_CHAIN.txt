1. SCOPE AND EXACT INPUTS

Let S,Y,Z,N be nonnegative real numbers, nu>0 and d real. Assume
  N^4 <= S^6 Y^3 Z^3,                              (F4)
  d + 2 nu Z <= 2N.                               (EN)
The intended physical identifications are Y=||grad u||_2^2,
Z=||Delta u||_2^2, N=absolute convection pairing, d=Y'. They
are not definitions of a supplied NSE solution or assumptions hidden in a
port; they remain separate physical realization obligations.

For a genuine H2 vector field, the intended spatial route is Holder,
||u||_6 <= S||grad u||_2,
||grad u||_3 <= ||grad u||_2^(1/2)||grad u||_6^(1/2),
||grad u||_6 <= S||D2 u||_2, and ||D2u||_2=||Delta u||_2.
These yield N <= S^(3/2)Y^(3/4)Z^(3/4), hence (F4).
This file does not prove their physical admission. Its closed chain begins
with the explicitly stated scalar (F4) and (EN).

2. EXACT POLYNOMIAL CERTIFICATE

For arbitrary real N,V,
 (3N-2V)^2 (3N^2+4NV+4V^2)
   = 27N^4 - 32NV^3 + 16V^4.
For N,V>=0 both factors on the left are nonnegative. Therefore
  32NV^3 <= 27N^4 + 16V^4.                        (P)
This is an exact rational polynomial identity, not a rounded estimate.

3. FOURTH-POWER YOUNG ESTIMATE

More generally let K>=0 and N^4<=K Z^3, with Z>=0, nu>0.
If Z=0, then N^4=0 and N=0, so the claim below holds.
If Z>0, put V=nu Z in (P) and substitute the fourth-power bound:
  32N nu^3 Z^3 <= 27K Z^3 + 16nu^4 Z^4.
Divide by the positive 16nu^3 Z^3 to obtain
  2N <= nu Z + 27K/(16nu^3).                      (Y)
No division by zero or positivity of Y is concealed.

4. CUBIC ENSTROPHY COEFFICIENT

Use K=S^6Y^3 in (Y), and combine with (EN):
  d + nu Z <= [27S^6/(16nu^3)]Y^3.               (C)
When the explicit sixth-power equality
  S^6=16/(27pi^4)                                (S6)
is supplied, arithmetic alone gives
  27S^6/(16nu^3)=1/(pi^4nu^3),
  d + nu Z <= Y^3/(pi^4nu^3),
  d <= Y^3/(pi^4nu^3).                           (Cpi)
Thus the registered pi^(-4)nu^(-3) coefficient is recovered exactly.
Equality (S6) is not a proof that S is an admissible Sobolev constant.
