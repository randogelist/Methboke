0. PRECISE ENDPOINT

Let C_i, i in Fin 6, be the six disjoint even Borel sectors whose union is
R3, including all coordinate ties and the origin. Let F be the unitary
Fourier transform with phase exp(-2*pi*i*x.xi). On complex-vector L2 define

  P_i f = 1_(C_i) f,
  Q_i^C = F^(-1) P_i F.

Construct actual bounded real-linear projections Q_i on the physical
H1(R3;R3) space, preserving its solenoidal subspace, such that

  sum_i Q_i u = u,
  partial_j(Q_i u) = Q_i(partial_j u),
  sum_i ||Q_i u||_H1^2 = ||u||_H1^2,
  sum_i ||grad(Q_i u)||_2^2 = ||grad u||_2^2.

On the right side of the derivative equation, Q_i denotes its L2 action.

1. REAL RANGE OF THE COMPLEX PROJECTIONS

Write c:L2(R3;R3)->L2(R3;C3) for componentwise complexification. This is a
real-linear isometry onto the closed subspace fixed by physical conjugation.
Write R for componentwise real part on complex L2. Both are well-defined on
a.e. classes, since their pointwise maps are continuous, norm-bounded and
send zero to zero. They satisfy R(cu)=u, and c(Rv)=v whenever v is real a.e.

On Fourier-side complex L2 define the conjugate-linear isometry
  J f(xi) = conjugate(f(-xi)).
Reflection preserves Lebesgue measure, so this is well-defined on classes.
For real Schwartz fields, the defining integral gives J(F(cu))=F(cu).
Density in real L2 and continuity of F,J,c extend the identity to every
real L2 field. For an arbitrary complex L2 field write
  v=c(Re v)+i*c(Im v).
Complex linearity of F and conjugate linearity of J, applied to the two
real fields, then give the full identity on all complex L2:
  F(conjugate(v)) = J(Fv).
The inverse implication follows from injectivity of F. Thus
  v is real a.e. <=> J(Fv)=Fv.                         (1.1)

Because C_i=-C_i exactly and its indicator is real,
  J(P_i f)=P_i(Jf).
For real u, P_i F(cu) is therefore J-fixed. By (1.1), Q_i^C(cu) is real
a.e. Define the actual real-L2 operator
  Q_i u = R(Q_i^C(cu)).                               (1.2)
Then c(Q_i u)=Q_i^C(cu). Real linearity, contraction, idempotence, mutual
annihilation, reconstruction and real self-adjointness follow from the
corresponding complex-L2 identities and the isometry c. These are
transported properties of the constructed operator, not a supplied
abstract projector record.

2. CONSTRUCT THE PHYSICAL WEAK GRADIENT

Take u in physical H1 and write G_j=partial_j u in real L2, j in Fin 3.
The actual forward compact-test Fourier theorem gives
  F(cG_j)(xi) = (2*pi*i*xi_j) F(cu)(xi) a.e.          (2.1)
Set

These fields are real L2 by section 1. Multiplying (2.1) by 1_(C_i) and
commuting two pointwise scalar multipliers gives

Only finitely many indices occur, so the equalities can be placed on a

Thus (v_i,H_i) belongs to the closed physical H1 graph, with no H2 or
Laplacian premise. This constructs Q_i on H1 and proves derivative

3. H1 BOUNDEDNESS, ORTHOGONALITY AND THE PHYSICAL CHARGES

Real-L2 contraction applied to u and every G_j yields
  ||Q_i u||_H1^2 = ||Q_i u||_2^2 + sum_j ||Q_i G_j||_2^2
                 <= ||u||_2^2 + sum_j ||G_j||_2^2
                  = ||u||_H1^2.
Hence the H1 action is a bounded real-linear operator. The physical H1
inner product is the sum of its L2 velocity and gradient inner products.
L2 self-adjointness and derivative commutation prove H1 self-adjointness.
Idempotence and annihilation hold for the underlying L2 velocity and hence
for the unique physical H1 element. Reconstruction follows the same way.

For i!=k, each of the velocity and gradient L2 inner products between
Q_i u and Q_k v is zero. Finite orthogonal-sum expansion therefore gives
  sum_i ||Q_i u||_H1^2 = ||u||_H1^2,
and, separately,
  sum_i sum_j ||Q_i G_j||_2^2 = sum_j ||G_j||_2^2.     (3.1)
Define the actual physical charges
  q_i(u)=||grad(Q_i u)||_2^2=sum_j ||Q_i G_j||_2^2.
They are nonnegative and satisfy
  sum_i q_i(u)=||grad u||_2^2.                        (3.2)

The lower-level statement (3.2) does not require the nonlinear PDE or an
energy identity. For a C_tH1 path on its existing lifetime, each q_i is continuous, since Q_i and
the physical gradient maps are continuous linear maps and squared norm
is continuous. This supplies actual measurable charges for the full
nonnegative time-budget comparison. This asserts neither continuity through

4. PRESERVATION OF SOLENOIDALITY

vector components. Consequently it commutes with coordinate extraction,
finite sums, and (on physical H1) each weak spatial derivative. First
identify the native compact-test solenoidal predicate with
  sum_j component_j(G_j)=0 in scalar L2.
Compact-test weak integration by parts gives distributional divergence

Thus div u=sum_j partial_j u_j=0 is an actual L2 equality. Then
  div(Q_i u)=sum_j Q_i(partial_j u_j)=Q_i(div u)=0.
Here Q_i on scalar L2 uses the same real symbol; coordinate-extraction
commutation follows either from its definition or componentwise Fourier
calculus. Thus the H1 operators restrict to the complete solenoidal H1
subspace, with all properties of sections 2--3 retained.
