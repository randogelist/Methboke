1. TIME REGULARITY OF THE ACTUAL VELOCITY ENERGY

The weak time derivative in (0.1) and the initial trace give the Bochner
identity
  u(t)=u(s)+integral_s^t u_t(r)dr                       (1.1)
in L2 for 0<=s<=t<=R. Thus u is absolutely continuous as an L2-valued
map on each such slab. The Hilbert norm-square chain rule gives
  E absolutely continuous,
  E'(t)=2<u(t),u_t(t)>_L2 for almost every t.             (1.2)
The pairing is integrable since u is bounded in L2 on a compact slab and
u_t is L2 in time. No pointwise spacetime smoothness is required.

2. ENSTROPHY AC WITHOUT ASSUMING A SPATIAL DERIVATIVE OF u_t

The claim
  Y absolutely continuous,
  Y'(t)=-2<Delta u(t),u_t(t)>_L2 a.e.                    (2.1)
requires a separate argument: (0.1) does not assert grad u_t in L2.

Let P_n be the spatial Fourier cutoff to |xi|<=n, acting on real fields
through its real/even indicator symbol, and set u_n=P_n u. It is a bounded
self-adjoint L2 operator commuting with weak spatial derivatives. For fixed
n, grad P_n is a bounded L2-to-L2 map. Therefore grad u_n is W^(1,2) in
time, with time derivative grad P_n u_t, and the ordinary Hilbert chain
rule plus spatial integration by parts gives
  Y_n(t):=||grad u_n(t)||_2^2,
  Y_n'=-2<Delta P_n u,P_n u_t>.                         (2.2)

P_n converges strongly to the identity on H1. This convergence is uniform
on the compact image u([0,R]) in H1, so Y_n->Y uniformly on [0,R].
Also
  Delta P_n u->Delta u in L2(0,R;L2),
  P_n u_t->u_t in L2(0,R;L2).
Cauchy-Schwarz implies convergence of the pairings in (2.2) in L1 time.
Passing in
  Y_n(t)=Y_n(0)+integral_0^t Y_n'(r)dr
gives (2.1) and its exact integrated identity at every t, including the
initial and final endpoints of the chosen compact slab. The construction
derives AC from the physical regularity; it does not assume Y is AC.

3. SPATIAL CANCELLATIONS ON R3

For almost every time u is actual H2 and solenoidal. The spatial estimates

3.1 Convection.
H1 interpolation gives u in L3 intersect L4. Hence |u|^3 and
|u|^2|grad u| are integrable. With a compact cutoff chi_L equal to one
on B_L, supported in B_(2L), and |grad chi_L|<=C/L, weak product rules and
div u=0 give
  integral chi_L u.B
    =-(1/2)integral |u|^2 u.grad chi_L.
The absolute value of the right side is bounded by
  (C/(2L)) integral_(L<|x|<2L)|u|^3 ->0.
The left side tends to <u,B>, since u.B is integrable. Therefore
  <u,B>=0.                                             (3.1)

3.2 Pressure.
An L2 distributional gradient is orthogonal to the solenoidal L2 subspace.
This follows from the actual Helmholtz/Fourier projection in the G01
construction. Both u and Delta u are solenoidal L2 at the times in question,
the second because divergence commutes with the spatial Laplacian.
Consequently
  <u,grad p>=0,  <Delta u,grad p>=0.                    (3.2)
This avoids assuming spatial decay of a pressure representative. Addition
of a function of time to p leaves these identities unchanged.

3.3 Dissipation.
Actual weak integration by parts, or Plancherel, gives
  <u,Delta u>=-Y.                                      (3.3)
All the preceding identities are on the same actual physical trajectory.

4. EXACT ENERGY AND ENSTROPHY IDENTITIES

Insert (0.1) into (1.2) and use (3.1)--(3.3):
  E'=-2nu Y a.e.,
  E(t)+2nu integral_s^t Y(r)dr=E(s)                    (4.1)
for every 0<=s<=t<=R. In particular 0<=E(t)<=M^2.

Insert (0.1) into (2.1), using pressure orthogonality against Delta u:
  Y'+2nu Z=2<B,Delta u> a.e.,
  Y'+2nu Z<=2N.                                       (4.2)
The sign is fixed by u_t=nu Delta u-B-grad p.
The pairings in (4.2) are integrable by the finite-slab L2 bounds above.

Because E is AC, E^2 is AC and
  (E^2)'=2EE'=-4nu EY.
Since E and Y are continuous on [0,R], their product is integrable, and
  E(t)^2+4nu integral_s^t E(r)Y(r)dr=E(s)^2.           (4.3)
At s=0 this supplies the exact weighted product budget
  integral_0^t EY=(M^4-E(t)^2)/(4nu)<=M^4/(4nu).        (4.4)
The factor one quarter comes from differentiating E^2; it is not the weaker
bound obtained merely by multiplying E<=M^2 into the dissipation budget.

5. CUBIC ENSTROPHY AND ONE FINITE FULL STOPPED BUDGET

6. STRICT SMALL-PRODUCT DISSIPATION: THE PHYSICAL THRESHOLD

A second spatial estimate, using different Holder exponents, is essential:
  N<=||u||_3 ||grad u||_6 ||Delta u||_2.
Interpolate the velocity between L2 and L6 and apply (0.2):
  ||u||_3<=||u||_2^(1/2)||u||_6^(1/2)
          <=S6^(1/2) E^(1/4)Y^(1/4).
Using ||grad u||_6<=S6||Delta u||_2 now gives
  N<=q Z,
  q:=S6^(3/2)(EY)^(1/4)>=0,  q^4=S6^6 EY.              (6.1)
Thus (4.2) implies
  Y'+2(nu-q)Z<=0.                                     (6.2)

Set W=EY and delta=nu^4/S6^6>0. Whenever W<delta, (6.1) gives q<nu.
This inference is strict: multiply by S6^6>0, compare nonnegative fourth
powers, then take the positive gap nu-q. It does not divide by W or Z.
Consequently on a subcritical time interval, Y'<=0 a.e. and E'<=0, so
  W'=E'Y+EY'<=0 a.e.                                  (6.3)

Suppose W(s)<delta for an actual interior time s. For any later compact
slab, choose a level theta with W(s)<theta<delta. If W ever reaches theta,
continuity supplies a first hitting time. Before that time W<=theta<delta,
so (6.3) and AC imply W at the hitting time is <=W(s), a contradiction.
Allowing any such theta, and then using AC while W<delta, yields
  W(t)<=W(s) for every s<=t<Tmax.                      (6.4)
The argument uses the derivative inequality only within the subcritical
region; it does not presuppose the desired invariant bound.

Put q_s=S6^(3/2)W(s)^(1/4)<nu. Since the fourth root is monotone, (6.4)
and (6.2) give the quantitative retained dissipation
  Y(t)+2(nu-q_s)integral_s^t Z(r)dr<=Y(s),
  E(t)<=E(s),  Y(t)<=Y(s) for all s<=t<Tmax.            (6.5)
This bounds the actual full H1 norm on the future trajectory. On the
earlier compact interval [0,s] it is already bounded by continuity.
The actual G01 interior restart therefore excludes any finite Tmax.
A strictly subcritical actual interior trace has global canonical evolution.
