1. EXACT ENDPOINT AND LOGICAL SCOPE

Fix nu > 0. Define NSE_A(nu) to mean:

  For every real divergence-free Schwartz datum a on R3, there exists a
  velocity-pressure pair (U,p) on [0,infinity) x R3, jointly smooth up to
  t=0, satisfying
    U_t + (U.grad)U + grad p = nu Delta U,
    div U = 0,
    U(0) = a,
  and sup_(t>=0) ||U(t)||_L2^2 < infinity.

This is the literal unforced smooth finite-energy category in Fefferman A.
No periodicity, mean-zero condition, Sobolev bound on the solution, or
prescribed pressure gauge is added to this proposition. [L01]

The intended conditional root is

  For every nu > 0,
    StoppedCommutatorBoundR3(nu) ==> not NSE_A(nu).                 (1.1)

The implication negates the universal existence assertion. It does not
prove positive global regularity, nor does it construct a datum satisfying
the stopped condition. If such a datum were later supplied, the conditional
root would exclude a literal global solution for that datum.

The single research premise in (1.1) will be the actual stopped inequality.
The approximation family, its uniqueness, energy and enstrophy identities,
and its stability against a hypothetical smooth solution belong in the
proof. They are not extra fields hidden inside the condition.

3. PHYSICAL NORMALIZATION AND SPATIAL ESTIMATES

All spatial integrals use Lebesgue measure on R3. Fibers carry Euclidean
norms, and gradient/Hessian arrays carry Frobenius norms. Write

  B(v,w) = (v.grad)w,
  ||v||_H1^2 = ||v||_2^2 + ||grad v||_2^2,
  ||v||_H2^2 = ||v||_2^2 + ||grad v||_2^2 + ||D2 v||_2^2.

Use the project Fourier convention
  Fv(xi) = integral exp(-2 pi i x.xi) v(x) dx.

For physical H2 fields, Plancherel and commuting weak derivatives give
  ||D2 v||_2^2 = ||Delta v||_2^2,
  ||grad v||_2^2 = integral (2 pi)^2 |xi|^2 |Fv(xi)|^2.
These normalizations match H2Spectral. [M03]

Choose fixed finite constants S > 0 and Cinf > 0 with
  ||f||_6 <= S ||grad f||_2,                                    (3.1)
  ||f||_infinity <= Cinf ||f||_H2.                              (3.2)

The first estimate is used also for finite-dimensional tensor fibers,
in particular f = grad w. No optimal constant is needed.

B-tier admission of (3.1):
  Start with the nonsharp scalar Sobolev estimate on compact C1 functions.
  Apply the norm/Kato argument to finite-dimensional fibers. Approximate
  a physical H1 field in H1 by compact smooth fields. The Sobolev estimate
  makes that sequence Cauchy in L6; identify its L6 limit with the L2
  representative using local convergence or a common a.e. subsequence.
  This supplies the actual H1-to-L6 map. It is not a supplied "Sobolev port".

Mathlib's compact-C1 eLpNorm estimate and the project's source cutouts are
concrete formalization inputs. The native extension to the physical graph
is a new adapter unless an exact accepted one is recovered. [A03, M11]

B-tier admission of (3.2):
  Let w2(xi) = 1 + (2 pi |xi|)^2 + (2 pi |xi|)^4.
  The integral of 1/w2 over R3 is finite: the integrand is bounded near
  zero and decays like |xi|^(-4) at infinity.
  Cauchy-Schwarz gives
    ||Ff||_1 <= (integral 1/w2)^(1/2)
                (integral w2 |Ff|^2)^(1/2)
              = Cinf ||f||_H2.
  Fourier inversion supplies the bounded continuous representative and
  its infinity norm estimate. This proves existence of a sufficient Cinf
  without introducing a sharp QFA numerical constant.

Combining (3.1), interpolation, and the Hessian-Laplacian identity gives
  ||grad w||_3 <= S^(1/2) ||grad w||_2^(1/2)
                             ||Delta w||_2^(1/2),               (3.3)
  ||B(w,w)||_2 <= S^(3/2) ||grad w||_2^(3/2)
                                ||Delta w||_2^(1/2).            (3.4)

All constants here are independent of the approximation index. The sharp
QFA constant, the cutoff tau_A, and universal six-charge finiteness are not
dependencies of the present obstruction.


4. THE ACTUAL SHARP CUTOFF AND COMPLETE STATE SPACE

For n in Nat, put N = n+1 and define
  m_n(xi) = 1 if |xi| <= N, and 0 otherwise,
  J_n v = F^(-1)(m_n Fv).

The symbol is measurable, real, even, and bounded by one. It therefore fits
the existing RealEvenSymbol interface without any smooth-symbol assumption.
Its discontinuity is harmless for the L2 multiplier construction. [M02]

Prove the following identities for the actual operators:

  J_n^2 = J_n,
  <J_n v,w> = <v,J_n w>,
  ||J_n v||_Hk <= ||v||_Hk for k=0,1,2,
  J_n partial_j = partial_j J_n on actual weak derivatives,
  J_n Delta = Delta J_n,
  J_n P = P J_n,                                                (4.1)

where P is the actual real Leray projection. Its symbol is an orthogonal
projection a.e.; its value at xi=0 does not affect the L2 operator.

Use the accepted gradient multiplier transport, then iterate it on the
H2 graph. Idempotence and self-adjointness follow from the real indicator
symbol and Fourier isometry, using the existing sector proofs as templates.
These ball-cutoff conclusions are new instances, not already existing
theorems under the sector names. [M02-M04]

Define
  E_n = {v in SpatialL2 : J_n v = v and P v = v}.

It is the intersection of kernels of the continuous linear maps I-J_n and
I-P. Hence it is a closed real Hilbert subspace of SpatialL2 and is complete.
Mathlib has the required closed-kernel/completeness infrastructure. [A01]

Important: E_n is infinite-dimensional. Frequencies in a Euclidean ball
are continuous. The periodic finite-dimensional Galerkin constructor does
not instantiate on this space.

Strong convergence:
  For v in Hk, k=0,1,2,
    ||(I-J_n)v||_Hk^2
      = integral_(|xi|>n+1) wk(xi) |Fv(xi)|^2 --> 0.             (4.2)

Here wk is the exact nonnegative Fourier weight for the physical norm.
The conclusion follows from integrability and dominated convergence.

Uniform convergence on a compact set K in H1:
  Given eta>0, choose a finite eta/4-net in K.
  Make ||(I-J_n)v_j||_H1 < eta/2 for each net point.
  Since ||I-J_n|| <= 2, every point of K then has error < eta.
Thus strong convergence becomes uniform on compact reference trajectories.
No compactness of an infinite-dimensional norm ball is used.


5. BANDLIMITED CALCULUS AND NONLINEAR LIPSCHITZ BOUNDS

For v in E_n, Fourier inversion supplies actual smooth representatives:
all frequency moments on the finite ball belong to L1 by Cauchy-Schwarz.
In particular,

  ||grad v||_2 <= 2 pi N ||v||_2,
  ||Delta v||_2 <= (2 pi N)^2 ||v||_2,
  ||v||_infinity <= cB N^(3/2) ||v||_2,                         (5.1)
  cB = sqrt(4 pi/3).

The last estimate uses the volume of the frequency ball and vector-valued
Fourier inversion. It has no decay-at-infinity hypothesis on v.

For u,v in E_n, let cN = 2 pi cB N^(5/2). Then
  ||B(u,v)||_2 <= cN ||u||_2 ||v||_2,
  ||B(u,u)-B(v,v)||_2
       <= cN (||u||_2+||v||_2) ||u-v||_2.                     (5.2)

Define the actual vector field
  F_(nu,n)(v) = nu Delta v - J_n P B(v,v).

It maps E_n to E_n by (4.1). For ||v||_2 <= R,
  ||F_(nu,n)(v)||_2
       <= nu (2 pi N)^2 R + cN R^2.                           (5.3)

For u,v in that ball,
  ||F_(nu,n)(u)-F_(nu,n)(v)||_2
       <= [nu (2 pi N)^2 + 2 cN R] ||u-v||_2.                (5.4)

These are the exact uniform-on-balls hypotheses needed by the Banach-space
Picard theorem. They are derived from the physical convolution product,
not postulated as an NSE solver interface.


6. CANONICAL GLOBAL REGULARIZED FLOW

6.1 Local construction

For each nu>0, n, and a in Schwartz_sigma, apply the Banach Picard theorem
to F_(nu,n) with initial value J_n a. P J_n a = J_n a by solenoidality.

Mathlib's
  IsPicardLindelof.exists_eq_forall_mem_Icc_hasDerivWithinAt
requires completeness, not finite dimension.
The time-independent constructor takes the ballwise bounds (5.3)-(5.4)
and the prescribed time-window size condition. [A02]

At any center with norm <= M0, use its radius-one ball, contained in the
origin-centered ball of radius M0+1. For example let
  L = nu (2 pi N)^2 (M0+1) + cN (M0+1)^2,
  K = nu (2 pi N)^2 + 2 cN (M0+1),
  h = min(1, 1/(2(L+1)), 1/(2(K+1))) > 0.                     (6.1)

The displacement bound is at most 1/2 and the usual Picard contraction
has slack. The same h works at every such center. This gives a quantitative
uniform restart interval without compactness of closed balls.

6.2 Nonlinear cancellation

For the smooth bandlimited fields, use spatial cutoffs chi_R that equal
one on the radius-R ball and satisfy ||grad chi_R||_infinity <= C/R.

For solenoidal b and fields w of the regularity used here,
  integral chi_R w.(b.grad)w
     = -(1/2) integral |w|^2 b.grad chi_R.

The boundary term tends to zero. For example, H1 fields belong to L3,
and the absolute value is at most
  C/(2R) ||b||_3 ||w||_3^2.
All interior trilinear expressions used below are integrable by Holder.

Thus
  <w,B(b,w)> = 0.                                             (6.2)

For the fixed-cutoff energy proof one can obtain L3 from the bandlimited
L2 and Linfinity estimates alone. General H1-to-L6 admission is not needed
to construct the regularized flow.

Sharp frequency cutoffs do not generally preserve Schwartz decay.
Equation (6.2) must therefore be justified by this cutoff argument,
physical weak-derivative density, or an equivalent legitimate proof.

6.3 Exact energy and global continuation

For the actual local solution,
  u_n' = F_(nu,n)(u_n),
  u_n(0)=J_n a,

the self-adjoint commuting projections disappear when paired with u_n.
The spatial Green identity and (6.2) give
  (1/2) d/dt ||u_n||_2^2 + nu ||grad u_n||_2^2 = 0.            (6.3)

Hence ||u_n(t)||_2 <= ||J_n a||_2 <= ||a||_2.
Use M0=||a||_2 in (6.1). Restart and patch uniquely on successive intervals
of the same positive length h, for every nonnegative time.

Alternatively, a bounded RHS makes the path Cauchy at any finite maximal
endpoint. Completeness supplies a limit, and the same local existence
theorem continues the path. Neither proof uses finite-dimensional compactness.

The result is a uniquely determined canonical flow
  (nu,a,n) |-> u_n : [0,infinity) -> E_n.                     (6.4)

It is C1 in time in E_n. Bounded derivative maps from E_n to every fixed
physical Hk give the corresponding spatial/time regularity. Endpoint
derivatives can be stated one-sided; scalar conclusions later use only
interior two-sided derivatives.

Reuse:
  SingularClosure.energy_hasDerivAt_of_solution and
  norm_le_of_forwardSolution_of_dissipative explicitly omit finite
  dimension. The existing forwardGlobalSolution_exists does not.
The latter's global construction must be adapted using (6.1), not applied
with an invalid FiniteDimensional instance. [M05]


7. PHYSICAL ENSTROPHY AND THE RECIPROCAL DRIFT

On the actual constructed trajectory define
  e_n(t) = (1/2)||grad u_n(t)||_2^2,
  Z_n(t) = ||Delta u_n(t)||_2^2,
  W_n(t) = <B(u_n(t),u_n(t)), Delta u_n(t)>_L2.

The canonical enstrophy is half of the repository's full gradient-energy
quantity. This factor must remain explicit in all adapters.

Because u_n and its time derivative have actual H2 regularity at fixed n,
differentiate the physical gradient norm. Using the H1-H2 Green identity
and removing J_n P against Delta u_n gives
  e_n'(t) = W_n(t) - nu Z_n(t).                              (7.1)

For eStar>0 define the reciprocal observable and its explicit drift:
  V_n(t) = eStar/(eStar+e_n(t)),
  c_n(t) = eStar/(eStar+e_n(t))^2 * (nu Z_n(t)-W_n(t)).

Since e_n>=0,
  0 < V_n(t) <= 1,                                           (7.2)
and the chain rule gives
  V_n'(t)=c_n(t)                                             (7.3)
at positive interior times.

Thus a negative drift c_n <= -gamma is equivalent to
  W_n - nu Z_n >= (gamma/eStar)(eStar+e_n)^2.                  (7.4)

This fixes the production/dissipation sign. The drift is an actual
directional/time derivative, not an arbitrary function carrying a bound.


8. THE SOLE STOPPED CONDITION

For fixed nu>0, define StoppedCommutatorBoundR3(nu) by existence of

  one a in Schwartz_sigma(R3;R3),
  one eStar>0,
  one gamma>0,
  R : Nat -> positive real with R_n -> infinity,
  one n0 in Nat,

such that, for every n>=n0 and every 0<=t<1/gamma,

  [for every s in [0,t], e_n(s)<R_n]
       ==> c_n(t)<=-gamma.                                   (8.1)

Every e_n and c_n is formed from the same canonical flow (6.4), with the
same a and nu. The eStar, gamma, and datum do not depend on n.

This is the R3 analogue of the accepted periodic quantifier structure.
It includes no:
  - arbitrary solution-family witness;
  - convergence or stability assumption;
  - supplied scalar derivative identity;
  - singular FDSN seed;
  - assumed failure of NSE-A.

Initial nonvacuity is available internally:
  e_n(0) <= (1/2)||grad a||_2^2.
Because R_n -> infinity, sufficiently large cutoffs start below the threshold.
The final scalar contradiction does not need a separate initial-bound
premise; its whole-slab bound will imply the full guard.

No witness for (8.1) is derived in this text. Energy dissipation alone does
not imply the required positive enstrophy-production lower bound (7.4).


9. THE PRECISE INTERMEDIATE REFERENCE CONTRACT

Fix 0<T<infinity. The comparison theorem will take a supplied actual
reference on [0,T] satisfying
  U in C([0,T];H2_sigma),
  U(0)=a in the actual physical class,
  U_t = nu Delta U - P B(U,U)                                (9.1)
in distributions in time, with its strong trace.

This is a temporary lemma hypothesis for deriving stability. It is not
the final definition of NSE_A.

The product map H2 x H2 -> L2 is continuous. Therefore the RHS of (9.1)
is continuous in L2. The Banach-valued fundamental theorem of calculus
upgrades the path to C1_t L2 with one-sided endpoint derivatives.

For each fixed n, J_n:L2->Hk is bounded for every finite k. Consequently
  v_n=J_n U and w_n=u_n-v_n
have C1_t H1 regularity and continuous H2 representatives. Differentiating
the H1 error norm below is justified pointwise in the slab interior.

The native derivation needs actual time distribution/Bochner identities,
not only a field record asserting a projected PDE without trace meaning.


10. EXACT PROJECTED ERROR AND VANISHING RESIDUAL

Set
  v_n = J_n U,
  rho_n = v_n-U,
  w_n = u_n-v_n,
  r_n = J_n P [B(v_n,v_n)-B(U,U)].

Subtract the projected reference equation from the regularized equation:
  w_n' = nu Delta w_n
         - J_n P [B(w_n,w_n)+B(v_n,w_n)+B(w_n,v_n)]
         - r_n,
  w_n(0)=0.                                                  (10.1)

Both w_n and Delta w_n lie in the range of J_n P. Thus that projection
can be removed from their inner products with the nonlinear terms.
The forcing sign in (10.1) is minus.

Let
  M = sup_(0<=t<=T) ||U(t)||_H2 < infinity,
  delta_n = sup_(0<=t<=T) ||(J_n-I)U(t)||_H1.

The continuous H1 image of a compact time interval is compact in H1.
Equation (4.2) and the finite-net argument give
  delta_n -> 0.                                             (10.2)

Using the identity
  B(v_n,v_n)-B(U,U)=B(rho_n,v_n)+B(U,rho_n),
Holder, (3.1)-(3.3), and H2 contraction, obtain
  ||r_n(t)||_2
    <= ||rho_n||_6 ||grad v_n||_3
          + ||U||_infinity ||grad rho_n||_2
    <= (S^(3/2)+Cinf) M ||rho_n(t)||_H1
    <= (S^(3/2)+Cinf) M delta_n.                             (10.3)

Thus the residual tends to zero uniformly in time in L2. Only uniform
H1 tails are required; reference H3 and uniform H2 tail estimates are
unnecessary. No cutoff-dependent Bernstein constant occurs in (10.3).


11. FULL H1 DIFFERENTIAL INEQUALITY, WITH EXPLICIT CONSTANTS

Suppress n temporarily and put
  D = ||w||_2^2 + ||grad w||_2^2,
  Y = ||grad w||_2^2,
  Z = ||Delta w||_2^2,
  A = S^(3/2) M,
  Nerr = B(w,w)+B(v,w)+B(w,v).

Testing (10.1) against w-Delta w gives
  (1/2)D' + nu(Y+Z)
     = -<w,Nerr+r> + <Delta w,Nerr+r>.                        (11.1)

The L2 self-transport and reference-transport terms vanish:
  <w,B(w,w)>=0,
  <w,B(v,w)>=0.

The remaining six terms have the following bounds:

  |<w,B(w,v)>| <= A ||w||_2 ||grad w||_2 <= (A/2)D.           (11.2)

  |<w,r>| <= (1/2)D + (1/2)||r||_2^2.                       (11.3)

  |<Delta w,B(v,w)>|
     <= (nu/8)Z + (2 Cinf^2 M^2/nu)Y.                       (11.4)

  |<Delta w,B(w,v)>|
     <= (nu/8)Z + (2 A^2/nu)Y.                              (11.5)

  |<Delta w,B(w,w)>|
     <= S^(3/2) Y^(3/4) Z^(3/4)
     <= (nu/8)Z + (54 S^6/nu^3)Y^3.                         (11.6)

  |<Delta w,r>|
     <= (nu/8)Z + (2/nu)||r||_2^2.                          (11.7)

For (11.2), use w in L6 and grad v in L3.
For (11.4), use v in Linfinity and grad w in L2.
For (11.5), use w in L6 and grad v in L3.
For (11.6), use (3.4) and the elementary Young bound
  a z^(3/4) <= eta z + 27 a^4/(256 eta^3),
with eta=nu/8.

Sum (11.2)-(11.7), multiply by two, and use Y<=D:
  D' + 2nu Y + nu Z
       <= alpha D + beta D^3 + Kforce ||r||_2^2,              (11.8)

where
  alpha  = 1 + A + (4/nu)(Cinf^2 M^2 + A^2),
  beta   = 108 S^6/nu^3,
  Kforce = 1 + 4/nu.

These quantities are finite, nonnegative, and independent of n. The
low-frequency error ||w||_2^2 is retained. No Poincare inequality on R3
has been inserted.

Define the strictly positive sequence
  epsilon_n
    = Kforce [(S^(3/2)+Cinf) M delta_n]^2 + 1/(n+1).           (11.9)

Then epsilon_n>0 and epsilon_n->0, including when the residual is zero.
Dropping the nonnegative dissipative terms from (11.8), and choosing
C=alpha+beta, gives the exact consumer form
  D_n' <= C(D_n + D_n^3) + epsilon_n,
  D_n(0)=0.                                                  (11.10)

The extra 1/(n+1) is intentional: the existing scalar consumer requires
eventual strict positivity, not merely nonnegativity, of epsilon.

13. THE DECISIVE INTERMEDIATE ENDPOINT: R3 E09

By the squared triangle inequality and H1 contraction,
  e_n(t)
    = (1/2)||grad(w_n+v_n)||_2^2
    <= ||grad w_n||_2^2 + ||grad v_n||_2^2
    <= D_n(t)+M^2.

Combine this with (12.1):
  There exists n1 such that for every n>=n1 and every t in [0,T],
    e_n(t) <= M^2+1.                                        (13.1)

14. LITERAL NSE-A TO THE REFERENCE CLASS

The final statement must preserve the literal solution category in Section 1.
A bare smooth L2 field on noncompact space need not have finite global
derivative norms. The PDE admission therefore requires an actual theorem.

Established literature inputs [L02]:
  Tao, Corollary 11.1: a complete almost-smooth finite-energy solution
    with H1 data belongs to X1 on its closed slab.
  Lemma 4.1(i) and Corollary 4.3: pressure normalization and H1 mild form.
  Theorem 5.4(i),(iv): strong traces and all Sobolev/time-derivative bounds
    for Schwartz data.
  Remark 11.2: terminal smoothness is required.

These are established analytical inputs, not native Lean declarations.

The adapter for this particular target is shorter than the repository's
full canonical-globality equivalence. [M12]

Start from a hypothetical literal global pair for the datum a.
Choose a finite S>T, for instance S=T+1. Restrict that pair to [0,S].

For arbitrary nu>0, make the explicit change
  tau=nu t,
  V(tau,x)=nu^(-1)U(tau/nu,x),
  q(tau,x)=nu^(-2)p(tau/nu,x).

The viscosity-one equation follows by dividing every physical term by
nu^2. The transformed datum a/nu is Schwartz and solenoidal; the closed
time interval is [0,nu S]; bounded L2 energy is preserved.

Apply the cited admission and normalization results on this actual
closed slab, then transport back. The normalized velocity and its time
derivative have bounded Hm norms, for every fixed m. Hilbert-valued FTC
therefore supplies a continuous H2 representative and the actual
projected equation required by (9.1).

For exact timewise identification, pair both the Sobolev representative
and the original literal field with an arbitrary compact smooth spatial
test. Both pairings are continuous in time; they agree a.e., hence at
every time, including 0 and S. Thus this is the same velocity and datum.

The original pressure may differ from the normalized pressure by a smooth
function of time. Only the normalized pressure has the asserted global
Sobolev properties; no L2 claim is made for an arbitrary additive constant.

The implication needed is only
  literal global smooth finite-energy solution
      ==> adequate H2 reference on each fixed closed slab.    (14.1)

No canonical unregularized maximal solution or bounded-H1 continuation
theorem is needed for this direction.

Native obligations behind (14.1) remain substantial:
  a. actual literal closed-slab predicates and viscosity transport;
  b. localized vorticity/enstrophy estimates and global assembly;
  c. vorticity-to-physical-H1/H2 reconstruction and time measurability;
  d. pressure normalization and the genuine mild identity;
  e. persistence and representative/trace identification.

A record with these conclusions as assumed fields would not close (14.1).


15. ONE-CUTOFF CONTRADICTION AND ROOT ASSEMBLY

Assume StoppedCommutatorBoundR3(nu), with witnesses a,eStar,gamma,R,n0.
Suppose NSE_A(nu). Apply its universal quantifier to that same a.

Set Tc=1/gamma>0. The literal pair restricted to [0,Tc+1] supplies the
reference on [0,Tc] by Section 14. Section 13 supplies Cbound=M^2+1
and n1 with
  e_n(t)<=Cbound for all n>=n1 and t in [0,Tc].

Since R_n->infinity, choose one n satisfying
  n>=n0, n>=n1, and R_n>Cbound.

For every t<Tc and every s in [0,t],
  e_n(s)<=Cbound<R_n.
Thus the stopped guard holds throughout the interval and c_n(t)<=-gamma.

Sections 7 and 8 give closed-slab continuity, the interior derivative
V_n'=c_n, V_n(0)<=1, and V_n(Tc)>0. Apply the accepted scalar descent:
  V_n(Tc) <= V_n(0)-gamma Tc <= 1-1=0,
contradiction.

Therefore (1.1) follows at B-tier level from the preceding construction
and the explicitly identified established literature inputs.

The accepted native scalar endpoint
  positive_terminal_contradiction
has precisely these hypotheses. The existing CanonicalBarrier proof
already uses the same eventual-threshold selection, but its actual
periodic family must be replaced by a new R3 adapter. [M07-M08]

No singular limit, first-hit limit, FDSN seed, or general weak-solution
existence theorem is used in this root argument.

19. OPTIONAL IDENTITIES AND EXCLUDED DEPENDENCIES

19.1 Six physical charges

Once u_n has been admitted to PhysicalH1Sigma, use the accepted six fixed
Fourier sectors Q_alpha and set
  q_(alpha,n)(t)=||grad Q_alpha u_n(t)||_2^2.

The actual orthogonal-sector identity gives
  sum_alpha q_(alpha,n)(t)=||grad u_n(t)||_2^2=2 e_n(t).         (19.1)

This is an exact interpretation of the same enstrophy. It supplies neither
a negative reciprocal drift nor a stopped-bound witness. The factor two
comes from the half-enstrophy convention of this target.

19.2 Pointwise commutator interpretation

On the cutoff phase space, the scalar observable
  V(v)=eStar/(eStar+(1/2)||grad v||_2^2)
is differentiable, and its directional derivative along F_(nu,n) is the
physical c_n evaluated on the trajectory. The elementary derivation rule
for transport and multiplication reproduces the accepted normalized
commutator algebra when instantiated on these functions.

This is a pointwise differential-operator identity. No infinite-dimensional
phase-space measure, self-adjoint Hamiltonian, unitary evolution, or full
Hilbert operator realization is supplied by this argument.

19.3 Work not required before this conditional root

  General nonzero-data QFA or WFA;
  general Leray-Hopf existence;
  the sharp Sobolev constant and exact QFA cutoff;
  canonical maximal unregularized H1 theory;
  full NSE-A finite-charge-class equivalence;
  universal membership in that class;
  forced-heat maximal regularity;
  a singular FDSN_R3 seed;
  full Hilbert-space Heisenberg/Ehrenfest operator realization.

The existing FDSN_R3 Seed->Family theorem remains a distinct conditional
result about a supplied singular seed and a varied forcing family.
It does not instantiate this unforced same-datum construction.

19.4 Precise stopping boundary

The B-tier route here invokes the established literature admission in
Section 14 explicitly. Without that input or its proof, the derivation
would stop at I13 and at exclusion of the supplied H2-reference category;
one could not conclude the literal NSE-A root.

In native implementation, any missing physical construction must remain
an open endpoint. It must not become an extra assumption inside the sole
stopped predicate, a custom axiom, or a strengthened definition of NSE_A.
