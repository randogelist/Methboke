NOTATION AND EXPLICIT PREMISES

Space: T3=R3/Z3, volume one. Viscosity nu>0; no forcing.
a is a real smooth divergence-free mean-zero field.
Pressure is periodic and has spatial mean zero.

For the actual maximal classical velocity u, write
  E(t) = ||u(t)||_{dot H1/2}^2,
  Y(t) = ||grad u(t)||_2^2,
  Z(t) = ||u(t)||_{dot H3/2}^2,
  W(t) = ||Delta u(t)||_2^2.
These are exactly the paper's E,Y,Z,W; in particular E is not L2 energy.

  Q_u(t) = sum_{k in Z3, k!=0} |k|^2 |u_hat(t,k)|^2,
  u_hat(t,k) = integral_T3 u(t,x) exp(-2*pi*i*k.x) dx,
  tau_Q(a,nu) = ||a||_2^4 / (8*pi^2*nu^5),
  L = min(Tmax,tau_Q),
  B_L(u) = integral_(0,L) Q_u(t)^2 dt.

All Fourier modes are included. The sum and integral are nonnegative extended
quantities. In the formal statement the minimum in ENNReal is taken before
conversion to a real time; it is finite because tau_Q is finite.

The general WFB hypothesis is B_L(u)<infinity. It is proved from smallness in
Steps 2-4 only. Steps 5-10 retain it as a premise for arbitrary smooth data.
No global solution, endpoint trace or global Fourier-budget bound is assumed.

TEN DEPENDENCY-ORDERED STEPS

1. CONSTRUCT THE LOCAL SOLUTION AND ITS UNIQUE MAXIMAL TRAJECTORY

For smooth data in an H1 ball, local existence and uniqueness hold on a common
positive interval depending only on nu and the H1 bound. The paper states a
restart time h_nu(M)=c0*nu^3/(1+M^4) for ||b||_H1<=M. Smooth-data persistence
and uniqueness construct one maximal pair (u,p) on [0,Tmax), Tmax in (0,infinity].

[S] Proposition 3.1, printed pp.3-4, Eqs.(3.1)-(3.2), and the maximal-flow
construction in Proposition 5.2. The local result itself does not require
small critical norm.

The formal proof constructs its own admissible uniform lifespan from Galerkin

2. IDENTIFY THE PAPER'S SMALL-DATA COERCIVITY MECHANISM

Fix admissible S6,S3,Ctr as in [S] Eq.(2.3), P=S6*S3, and put
  h0=||a||_{dot H1/2}, delta=nu-P*h0.
On the SMALL branch assume delta>0. The estimates
  Y^2 <= E*Z,
  |<B(u,u),Lambda u>| <= P*sqrt(E)*Z
imply
  E' + 2*(nu-P*sqrt(E))*Z <= 0.
The first-exit argument gives E(t)<=h0^2, hence
  E' + 2*delta*Z <= 0
throughout the already existing maximal interval.

[S] Lemma 4.1, Lemma 4.2, Eqs.(4.1)-(4.3),(4.6).
The publication's Appendix C, P06/P07, records finite-Galerkin fixed-constant
formal counterparts. No new arbitrary-constant Lean audit is claimed here.

3. EXTRACT A COMMON QUARTIC-GRADIENT BUDGET BEFORE GLOBALIZATION

Multiply by 2E>=0 and use Y^2<=E*Z:
  (E^2)' + 4*delta*Y^2 <= 0.
For every 0<=b<Tmax,
  E(b)^2 + 4*delta*integral_0^b Y(t)^2 dt <= h0^4.
In particular the same bound h0^4/(4*delta) works for every shorter interval.
If L=0, the open-interval budget is zero. If L>0, monotone convergence as
nonnegative b increases to L gives
  integral_(0,L) Y(t)^2 dt <= h0^4/(4*delta).
This passage uses one common bound; finiteness on separate compact intervals
without a common bound would not suffice. No global existence conclusion is
used to justify this budget.

[S] Lemma 4.2, Eq.(4.4); Theorem 2.2, Eq.(2.10).
Appendix C P08/P12/P13 distinguishes the formal Galerkin and physical-limit
versions of the quantitative estimate.

4. TRANSLATE TO THE FULL FOURIER BUDGET AND STATE THE GENERAL HYPOTHESIS

Physical Parseval on the unit torus gives
  Y(t)=4*pi^2*Q_u(t),
  integral_(0,L) Y^2 = 16*pi^4*B_L(u).
Thus the SMALL branch has the explicit certificate
  B_L(u) <= h0^4 / (64*pi^4*delta).

For arbitrary smooth a, Steps 5-10 instead assume B_L(u)<infinity directly.
There is no inference from the small-data theorem to budget finiteness for
all smooth data. Smallness is one sufficient way to verify this hypothesis.

5. REUSE THE ENSTROPHY ESTIMATE WITHOUT A SMALLNESS PREMISE

The paper's Lemma 5.1 applies to every smooth solution:
  Y' + nu*W <= K*nu^(-3)*||u||_6^4*Y.
Together with ||u||_6<=S6*sqrt(Y), it gives
  Y' + nu*W <= C*nu^(-3)*Y^3,
where C=K*S6^4 is independent of the datum and its size.

The formal scalar dissipation variable is sqrt(W), so nu*z^2=nu*W.

6. OBTAIN ONE H1 BOUND ON THE WHOLE BUDGET INTERVAL

Put M_Y=16*pi^4*B_L(u)<infinity. The cubic inequality can be written as a
linear inequality for Y with coefficient C*nu^(-3)*Y^2. Gronwall therefore gives
  Y(t) <= Y(0)*exp(C*nu^(-3)*M_Y),   0<=t<L.
This is derived first on each closed b<L and uses the same M_Y for all b.
The zero case uses no division by Y(0). Mean-zero Poincare gives
  ||u(t)||_H1^2
    <= (1+(2*pi)^(-2))*Y(t)
    <= 2*Y(0)*exp(C*nu^(-3)*M_Y).
Thus one bound controls every pre-endpoint slice in the budget interval.

7. TURN THE H1 BOUND INTO ACTUAL CONTINUATION

Given a finite positive front [0,T) with the actual integral of Y^2 finite,
Step 6 yields one finite bound M for the H1 norm itself (take the square
root of its squared-norm bound). Step 1 provides a common restart duration
h_nu(M)>0. Choose s<T so close to T that s+h_nu(M)>T.
Restart from the smooth datum u(s). Uniqueness identifies velocity and
normalized pressure on the overlap. Gluing yields an actual longer front
[0,S), S>T, preserving both original fields throughout [0,T).
No solution value or regularity at T is assumed.

8. PROVE STRICT OVERLAP WITH THE QFB CUTOFF

Suppose Tmax<=tau_Q. Then Tmax is finite and L=Tmax, so the assumed Fourier
budget covers the whole open maximal interval. Steps 6-7 produce an extension
beyond Tmax, contradicting maximality. Therefore
  tau_Q(a,nu) < Tmax.
The argument excludes equality as well as an earlier endpoint. If tau_Q=0,
the strict inequality follows already from Tmax>0; infinite Tmax is included.

9. ATTACH THE QFB TAIL OF THE SAME ORIGINAL WEAK TRAJECTORY

Choose finite times tau_Q<d<eta<T<Tmax. Q01 supplies one original Leray-Hopf
witness v,G with datum a. On every compact classical interval, finite weak-strong
comparison identifies that same v with u. Q09 applied to that very witness
supplies a classical tail (w,q) after d.
At each time in the overlap, v=u and v=w spatially almost everywhere.
Spatial continuity gives
u=w pointwise. The equations imply grad(p-q)=0; both pressure means are zero,
so p=q. Splicing at eta is smooth because the pairs agree on an open overlap.
The resulting single global pair (U,P) preserves the entire original maximal
path, by the same-path comparison and uniqueness.

10. CONCLUDE THE EXACT WFB THEOREM

Every finite restriction of (U,P) is now an actual initial classical front.
Maximality bounds all such durations by Tmax, forcing Tmax=infinity. Hence
  B_min(Tmax,tau_Q)(u)<infinity
    implies Tmax=infinity and GlobalSmoothNSE(nu,a,U,P),
with U(t)=u(t) and P(t)=p(t) at every original nonnegative pre-endpoint time.
Since Tmax=infinity, this preserves the whole nonnegative trajectory.

THE PRECISE PUBLICATION-TO-WFB RELATION

Let S_nu={a in C-infinity_sigma,0 : P*||a||_{dot H1/2}<nu}.
Let W_nu={a in C-infinity_sigma,0 : B_min(Tmax(a),tau_Q(a,nu))(u_a)<infinity},
where u_a is the uniquely constructed maximal classical velocity.
Let R_nu^smooth be the smooth data admitting a global smooth normalized NSE pair.

  S_nu subseteq W_nu subseteq R_nu^smooth.

The first inclusion has the explicit certificate from Step 4. The second is
WFB. No strictness of these inclusions is claimed here. The paper's rough
small-H1/2 theorem and its strong initial trace remain a separate result.

The main distinction is between GENERATING the budget (Steps 2-4, using strict
smallness) and USING the budget (Steps 5-10, with finiteness explicit). QFB gives
the global tail after the explicit cutoff; continuation supplies the earlier
segment and strict overlap. Universal Fourier-budget finiteness remains open.
